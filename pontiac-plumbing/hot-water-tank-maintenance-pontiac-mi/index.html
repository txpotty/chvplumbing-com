    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Pontiac

    Hard water leaves calcium carbonate behind every time the burner heats the tank. Over a year that layer can harden into chunks that insulate the water from the flame and cause loud popping. We connect a hose to the drain valve, flush with full line pressure and agitate the bottom until the water runs clear. When scale is severe we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems. A clean tank heats faster and puts less stress on the bottom seam.

    Anode Rod Inspection & Replacement in Pontiac, MI

    The anode rod is your hot water tank's sacrificial protector against corrosion. Well water across much of the state tends to be harder and often carries iron, which stains sinks and clogs aerators. During our maintenance, we pull and inspect the anode rod. If it's badly depleted, we recommend and perform a replacement to extend the life of your tank in Pontiac, MI, preventing rust from eating away at the tank lining.

    Standard Hot Water Tank Flushing in Pontiac

    Sediment buildup is a frequent issue in hot water tanks, especially where water hardness runs high. Our standard flushing service removes these mineral deposits, which can reduce efficiency and lead to premature tank failure. We drain the tank completely, flush out all sediment and refill it, ensuring clear water flow and improved heating performance. This routine service is crucial for extending the life of your unit.

    Gas Burner and Venting Tune-Up in Pontiac, MI

    On gas tanks we clean the burner, inspect the thermocouple or flame sensor and look for soot, rust flakes or scorching around the combustion chamber. We check the draft hood and vent connector for proper slope and secure joints. Dust and lint from garages can block the flame arrestor screen on newer FVIR models, which causes the pilot to drop out. A clean burner gives a steady blue flame and lower gas bills.

    Standard Hot Water Tank Tune-Up in Pontiac

    CHV Plumbing offers a complete tune-up for conventional hot water tanks, focusing on critical components. This includes draining the tank to remove sediment buildup, which is crucial wherever water carries minerals, and inspecting the anode rod for signs of corrosion. We also check heating elements, thermostat settings and all pipe connections for leaks. Our goal is to improve your tank's performance and prevent future issues.

    Pressure Relief Valve Testing in Pontiac

    A properly functioning temperature and pressure relief (T&P) valve is a crucial safety device for any hot water tank. Our technicians will test this valve to confirm it's not stuck or leaking, preventing dangerous pressure buildup within the tank. We verify it opens and closes correctly, and if it fails, we will suggest immediate replacement to keep your Pontiac home safe and compliant with plumbing codes.

    Electric Element and Thermostat Service in Pontiac

    Electric tanks rely on upper and lower heating elements that get crusted with scale in hard water. We test each element with a meter, check the thermostats and look for scorched wires at the terminal block. If an element has failed we replace it with the matching wattage and voltage. Many families who think they need a new heater just need a lower element and a flush.

    Over time, minerals and sediment settle at the bottom of hot water tanks, reducing efficiency and possibly leading to premature failure. Our maintenance includes a thorough flushing of the tank to remove these deposits.     Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Pontiac

    The cost of hot water tank maintenance in Pontiac, MI generally ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age and specific services required. This usually includes flushing, anode rod inspection and safety valve checks. We provide upfront pricing before any work begins.

    In mineral-heavy water a magnesium rod may last three to five years. We measure it at every visit so you replace it before the tank lining starts to rust.

    Watch for slower recovery, rusty or cloudy hot water, noises, moisture around the base or a relief valve that drips. Any of these means it is time for an inspection visit.

    Many manufacturers, including Rheem, A.O. Smith and Bradford White, expect regular maintenance and can deny claims for sediment damage. Keeping service records protects your warranty.

    That smell usually comes from bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. We can switch to an aluminum-zinc or powered anode and sanitize the tank to clear it up.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, which matters most when water carries a lot of minerals, and it extends the lifespan of your unit.

    Routine hot water tank maintenance, such as flushing and inspections, usually does not require a permit in Pontiac. However, if major components are replaced or the entire unit is swapped out, a plumbing permit may be necessary. Our team takes care of all necessary permit applications.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to poor operation, higher energy bills, premature tank failure from corrosion or sediment buildup, and potential safety hazards from a malfunctioning pressure relief valve. Regular service heads off these costly and dangerous issues.
