    Burst Copper Pipe Repair in Pontiac, MI

    Copper in an exterior wall or attic can split when the water inside freezes. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and poorly insulated additions are the most common places for a freeze to cause a break. Close the main valve. The split often does not leak until the ice thaws, which is why many bursts are found hours after the cold. We cut out the split and solder in new pipe. Insulation goes on after.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Repair in Pontiac

    Pinholes form when the inner wall of copper pipe erodes or pits. Hot lines get it worst. They often appear in thin Type M pipe and on hot water lines where velocity and temperature are higher, so we cut out the affected section and replace it with Type L copper or a press fitting. More pinholes often follow.

    Underground Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Pontiac

    Buried copper service lines can leak for months before anyone notices. The meter gives it away. We locate the leak with acoustic gear and tracer gas, then repair it through a small pit or replace the line by pulling new PEX or Type K copper through the old path. Every repair gets a pressure test.

    Commercial Copper Pipe Leak Services in Pontiac, MI

    Hot Water Copper Line Repair in Pontiac, MI

    Hot water lines corrode faster than cold lines. Temperature drives it. Recirculation pumps that run around the clock push water through the copper at high velocity and wear it from the inside, which is why we often add a timer to the pump after fixing the leak. It slows the next pinhole.

    Start with water stains on walls and ceilings. Look under sinks next. Green or blue corrosion on fittings, musty odors, a higher bill and the sound of dripping inside walls all point to a copper leak, and low pressure at one fixture can mean a leak on that branch. A visit settles it.

    A repair clamp or epoxy can buy time in an emergency. It will not last. A plumber should cut out the damaged section and install proper fittings, especially inside walls where a failed patch can rot framing before you notice. Shut the water off first.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Not the pipe itself. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Ask your insurer. We provide documentation.
