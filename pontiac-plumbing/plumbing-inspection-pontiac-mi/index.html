    Leak Detection and Assessment in Pontiac, MI

    Hidden leaks waste water and quietly damage floors and walls. Start at the meter. We shut everything off, check the meter's leak dial and then follow the leak with acoustic equipment and thermal imaging. Early detection limits the damage. Freeze and thaw cycles move the soil around buried pipes, which can crack older clay and cast iron sewer laterals.

    Preventive Maintenance Checks in Pontiac, MI

    A maintenance check is a lighter version of a full inspection for owners who want to stay ahead of problems. We flush the water heater, exercise the main shutoff and angle stops, check pressure and look for slow drains and weeping fittings. It takes about an hour. Doing this every year or two adds years to a water heater and keeps the shutoffs working for the day you really need them.

    Whole-Home Plumbing Inspections in Pontiac

    Our whole-home inspections focus on the problems we see most in Michigan houses. That list starts with old supply pipe, worn angle stops, aging water heaters, slow drains and sewer lines with roots or sags. We test each one directly. Catching a corroded fitting or a partial sewer blockage now keeps it from becoming a ceiling stain or a backup on a holiday weekend.

    Sewer Line Camera Inspection in Pontiac, MI

    Older homes in Pontiac often have cast iron or clay sewer pipe that cracks, scales up or lets roots in at the joints. Our camera shows each defect, and the onboard counter tells us exactly how far from the cleanout it sits. We mark it on the ground. Knowing the depth and location means a repair estimate can be accurate, and it helps decide between a spot repair, a liner or a full replacement.

    Fixture, Drain and Shutoff Valve Check in Pontiac, MI

    We run every faucet, shower and toilet and look for slow drains, leaks and weak flow. We test each valve. Angle stops and the main shutoff valve often seize over the years, and we also listen for gurgling drains that point to venting problems, since small fixes like these are easy to plan once you know about them.

    Gas Connection Safety Check in Pontiac, MI

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. We check for old uncoated brass connectors, missing shutoffs, missing sediment traps and corrosion, and we test joints with an electronic detector. Any leak is reported on the spot. Older homes are where we find the most issues, usually at appliance connectors that have been there since the house was built.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Pontiac

    Standard plumbing inspections typically run $150 to $300. With the sewer camera, most totals land between $250 and $500.

    Our full inspection covers supply pipe, drains, fixtures, shutoff valves, the water heater, gas connectors and the sewer line. Pressure gets measured. We also run a meter leak test, look for signs of slab leaks and hard water damage and check any softener or filter system tied into the plumbing.

    Looking for drips and watching the meter is worth doing on your own. It just goes only so far. A professional inspection uses cameras, pressure gauges and leak detection tools to check the parts you cannot see, including the sewer line and the pipe inside walls, which is where the expensive problems usually hide.

    For an older home, it usually pays for itself. Old pipe hides problems. Homes with galvanized supply pipe, clay or cast iron sewers or original water heaters often have problems that are invisible from the outside, and an inspection lets you plan repairs instead of reacting to a failure.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Camera work takes longer. Big houses, long sewers or a leak that needs tracking can stretch it, and we give you a time estimate when you schedule.

    Every one to two years works for most homes. Some houses need it yearly. Houses with original pipe, big trees over the sewer or past leaks do better with a yearly check that catches problems while they are small.

    A general home inspector checks basic function, while a plumber goes deeper with sewer cameras, pressure tests and pipe identification. Many buyers hire both.

    We identify supply pipe materials including polybutylene, Kitec, galvanized, copper, CPVC and PEX. Known risks and options go in the report.
