    Corroded Gas Pipe Replacement in Westland

    Buried steel gas line corrodes once its coating cracks, and moisture, roots and ground movement speed it up. Freeze and thaw cycles shift the soil around buried pipes, which can crack older clay and cast iron sewer laterals. Rusted buried steel gets replaced with polyethylene and tracer wire, and we use a listed transition riser where it rises to the house. Above-ground runs stay in steel. Everything is tested before the trench is backfilled, so a bad joint gets caught while it is still easy to reach.

    Interior Black Iron Pipe Repair in Westland

    Threaded black iron pipe can leak at joints after years of vibration or settling. We tighten, reseal or replace fittings and sections with new pipe and gas-rated thread sealant. Rusted pipe goes too. Pipe in a garage, crawlspace or exterior wall that shows heavy rust gets replaced before it turns into a leak, and the whole system gets a pressure test after the repair.

    CSST Repair and Bonding in Westland, MI

    Newer homes often have corrugated stainless steel tubing running through the attic. Nails, rodents and lightning can all damage CSST, and older yellow-jacket tubing must be bonded to the home's grounding system to lower arc risk. Bonding is a short job. Damaged runs get spliced with the manufacturer's fittings, and we confirm the bond on the way out. Spring snowmelt and a rising water table keep sump pumps running hard, so a backup pump or battery system is worth considering.

    Pool Heater, Fire Pit and Grill Lines in Westland, MI

    Backyard gas lines for pool heaters, fire pits and built-in grills face sun, weather and irrigation water that shorten their life. Pool heaters are the big load, often 250,000 to 400,000 BTU, so a line that was fine for a grill can starve the heater and the house appliances at the same time. Sizing is the first thing we check. New and repaired lines get tested, buried to the required depth and laid with tracer wire.

    Gas Line Extensions and Relocations in Westland

    Adding a gas fireplace, moving a laundry room or switching a range from electric to gas usually means extending the gas line. Sizing comes first. Our plumbers total the BTU load, measure the run and size the pipe so the last appliance on the line still gets full pressure at peak use. Then we route it, tie it in and test it.

    Gas piping should be repaired by a licensed professional. Mistakes can cause fires, explosions or carbon monoxide problems, and DIY work may not pass inspection.

    Watch for a sulfur or rotten egg smell, a hiss near a pipe or appliance, visible damage to gas piping or dying plants near an outdoor line. Get outside right away. Once outside, call the gas company and then us, because the utility has to secure the meter before a plumber can safely start looking for the leak.

    Leaks at the meter or on the utility's service line belong to the gas company. Do not try to fix it yourself. Leave the property and call the utility, and after they address their side we can fix anything on your side of the meter.

    There is no required yearly inspection for most homes. Older homes benefit from a check every few years. Old brass connectors, decades-old buried steel and appliances with yellow or weak flames are all good reasons to have the system checked.

    Look for dead or yellow grass in a line across the yard, bubbling in wet soil, a hissing sound or a rising gas bill. Any of those can point to a buried leak.
