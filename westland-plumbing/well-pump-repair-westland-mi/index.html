    Low Water Pressure Repair in Westland

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Westland

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. These pumps can also leak at the shaft seal or wear out an impeller. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Westland

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. Testing starts at the surface. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Westland, MI

    Electrical faults cause a big share of well calls, from a tripped breaker to a burned contact in the pressure switch. After that, we look at the capacitor and relay in the control box. We lock out the power before testing anything. All wiring and splices meet Michigan electrical code before the pump restarts.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Westland, MI

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets Michigan code before we restart the pump.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Westland

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, a clogged sensing tube and ant nests are the usual reasons one fails. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Repair in Westland

    Expect $300 for a pressure switch and up to $2,000 or more for a submersible motor replacement. Depth is the big variable. Deeper wells need more pipe and wire and more time on the hoist, which raises the cost of the same pump.

    A waterlogged pressure tank is the most common cause. A clogged sediment filter, a failing switch, a leak in the drop pipe or a falling water level can do it too. We test each one.

    Submersible pumps commonly last 8 to 15 years, depending on run time, sand and water quality. Short cycling from a bad pressure tank can cut that down a lot.

    Simple fixes like a pressure switch take a few hours at most. Replacing a submersible pump can take a full day, especially in a deep well.

    Start with the switch and the filter. A clogged filter or a low cut-in setting causes a lot of weak pressure complaints. If those check out, a larger pressure tank, a constant pressure system or a booster pump may be the answer, and we can tell you which fits your well's yield.

    Like-for-like pump replacement usually does not require a permit, but deepening or modifying the well falls under state well rules. Michigan enforces a statewide plumbing code based on the International Plumbing Code, with plumbers licensed by the state. We tell you if any filings are needed.

    Resetting a breaker or checking the pressure switch is reasonable for a homeowner. Pulling a pump involves heavy pipe, high voltage and contamination risk, so leave that to a pro with a hoist.
