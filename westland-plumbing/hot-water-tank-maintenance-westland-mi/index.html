    What a Tank Service Visit Covers in Westland, MI

    Expansion Tank Service in Westland, MI

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. We test the air side with a tire gauge. Precharge should match your house water pressure. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Westland, MI

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. We also check the thermocouple or flame sensor, since a weak one lets the pilot go out every few days and leaves you resetting it on your knees. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Anode Rod Replacement in Westland, MI

    Water chemistry changes from one street to the next, and so does anode life. Three to five years is typical. A softened water supply can chew through a magnesium rod in two. Swapping a $40 to $80 rod is a lot cheaper than a new tank, and on a tank under eight years old it is usually the best money you can spend on it.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Westland

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. We match the new valve to the heater's input rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Relief Valve Testing in Westland

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. We lift the test lever and watch it reseat. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Westland, MI

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. Attic tanks need a working pan. We check that the pan drain line runs to a safe spot and is not cracked or plugged, because one slow leak over a bedroom can bring down a ceiling. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Commercial Tank Maintenance in Westland, MI

    Restaurants, salons, laundromats and small offices run tanks much harder than a household does, often 75 to 100 gallons cycling all day. Sediment builds up two or three times faster. Most commercial tanks go on a quarterly or twice-yearly schedule with a dated log on the jacket. Downtime gets scheduled outside business hours.

    Standard Tank Flushing in Westland

    Flushing is the one job every tank needs, gas or electric, new or old. The harder the water, the sooner it matters. We drain the tank completely, refill it partway, drain it again and repeat until the hose runs clear, which on a heavily scaled tank can take four or five cycles. Then we close the valve and check that it reseals without a drip.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Westland

    Expect $75 to $200 on top of the flush for a new anode, parts and labor together. A powered anode runs higher. A seized rod that needs extra time with the impact gun, or a flexible link rod for a tank under a low ceiling, can push it toward the top of that range.

    All the major ones, including Rheem, Ruud, AO Smith, Bradford White, State, American and Kenmore-branded tanks. Parts are easy to get for all of them. Store-brand tanks are usually made by the same few factories.

    Rumbling is the deeper cousin of popping, and it means a thick bed of sediment is shifting and boiling on the bottom of the tank each time the burner lights. It gets louder over time. Electric tanks rumble less, but they crust up the lower element instead.

    Yearly, for most homes. Every six months is better on very hard water. Manufacturers like Rheem and Bradford White list annual flushing in their owner manuals, and a dated service record helps if you ever file a sediment-related warranty claim.

    Plan on 45 to 90 minutes. Thick scale adds time. Tight closets, attics and seized anodes each add a little.

    Sediment hardens, the burner or elements work harder, and the bottom of the tank runs hotter than it should. You lose efficiency first. After that the anode runs out, corrosion starts on the liner, and the usual ending is a leak from the bottom seam on a tank that might have lasted another five years.

    Slower recovery after a shower is the most common early sign. Rust-tinted water from the hot tap is another. Moisture on the floor around the base, a relief valve pipe that drips, or water pooling in the drain pan all mean the visit should happen this week rather than next season.

    No permit is needed for flushing, anode replacement or a thermostat adjustment in most places. Replacing the whole tank is different.
