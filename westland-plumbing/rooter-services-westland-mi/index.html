    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Westland

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. After-hours calls cost more.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots follow that moisture to the gap and push in. Once in, they grow quickly. In Westland, clay pipe with a joint every 2 to 3 feet and older cast iron with oakum joints are the most common targets.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Tough lines often get both, rooting to open the path and jetting to clean out what remains.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go many years without it. A camera look can set the timing. How quickly roots return after the last cut tells us if yearly or every-other-year service fits your line.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the common signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. That one is serious. Repeat clogs in one line usually mean a deeper problem, often roots at a joint well out in the yard.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. A cable can kink and wrap a hand or wrist fast. Rentals jam in pipes. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.
