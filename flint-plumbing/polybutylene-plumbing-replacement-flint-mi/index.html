    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Flint, MI

    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Flint

    Most poly replacements in Flint fall between $4,000 and $15,000 or more. Size drives the price. Small homes with one bath and an open attic cost the least, and big multi-bath houses or copper jobs cost the most. The quote comes in writing at the visit.

    Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. Scale builds inside. Scale buildup can reduce flow and add stress to pipe that is already brittle, so in hard water areas we often suggest adding a softener loop during the replacement.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. We use Uponor most often. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.

    We provide a written workmanship warranty on the repipe, and pipe makers offer their own long-term warranties. You get the details before we start.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. It is most common in homes from the late 1970s to mid 1990s.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Hot lines and fittings usually go before the rest.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. Age is another. A house from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe and a past fitting leak should be replaced before the next failure.
