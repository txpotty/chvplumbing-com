    Sewer Line Leak Detection in Flint, MI

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. We use a camera for these. Our tech runs a camera from the cleanout to the city tap, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, so you see the exact distance and depth of the break.

    Leak Testing for Multifamily and Offices in Flint

    Multifamily buildings and office parks have long supply systems where leaks hide for a long time. We isolate each unit or floor, test it and trace leaks with the same acoustic and thermal tools we use in single-family houses. Every unit gets its own report. Early detection means fewer tenant complaints and fewer insurance claims building-wide.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Flint, MI

    Copper leaks are loud on a ground microphone. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. Both show up on our schedule every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX top the list of what we find.

    Fixture and Appliance Leaks in Flint, MI

    One running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons daily. Worn faucet cartridges, softener valves and water heater connectors add more. We dye test tanks, check flappers and fill valves, and inspect supply lines and water heater fittings, with most of those fixed in under an hour from truck stock. They are inexpensive repairs.

    Pool Leak Detection in Flint

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation probably has a leak. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, listen for leaks with a hydrophone underwater, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain so we can point to the exact fitting or crack.

    Gas Leak Detection in Flint

    A faint rotten-egg odor by the furnace or stove needs a professional check. Leave the house if the odor is strong. Our licensed plumbers use a combustible gas detector and soap solution on fittings, appliance connectors and the meter riser, then pressure test the line to find a leak too small to smell, with repairs made to the fuel gas code before the gas comes back on.
