    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Flint

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and restrict flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives accurate readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Flint, MI

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are common causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to suit your new tank's pre-charge.

    Large Capacity Tanks for Ranch and Commercial Wells in Flint

    Properties with livestock, irrigation or more than one home on a well need more storage than a single residential tank. We install multiple tanks manifolded together or large capacity models over 100 gallons. Proper sizing keeps big three-phase pumps from cycling too often. We also check the check valves and pressure ratings on the distribution lines. Work is scheduled to keep water downtime brief for animals and occupants.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Flint

    Bladder tanks hold water inside a flexible butyl bag surrounded by pressurized air. When the bag ruptures, water fills the whole tank, there is no air cushion and the pump short cycles. A quick test is pressing the air valve on top, since water spitting out means the bladder has failed. We replace the tank, set the new pre-charge and confirm the pump runs a full cycle. Most bladder tank swaps take about two to three hours.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Flint, MI

    Diaphragm tanks like the Amtrol Well-X-Trol use a fixed membrane that separates air above from water below. They are durable, but the steel shell can rust through at the base after years of condensation in a hot, damp equipment space. We replace corroded diaphragm tanks and check the stand and fittings under them. The new tank is leveled and supported so it does not stress the piping. This restores steady pressure to every faucet.
