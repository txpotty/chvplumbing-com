    We install Kohler, TOTO, American Standard, Gerber and most other brands sold at supply houses and home centers. Each one has its own parts quirks.     Smart Toilet Installation in Flint, MI

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most require a GFCI outlet within reach of the cord.     Two-Piece Toilet Installation in Flint

    Two-piece toilets come with the tank and bowl separate, joined by a rubber gasket and two or three bolts. We assemble them on the floor before setting. Tightening the tank bolts evenly, side to side, matters more than how hard they go, because an uneven pull cracks the china or leaves a slow drip at one bolt. It should not wobble.

    Toilet Replacement and Upgrades in Flint, MI

    Swapping an old 3.5 gallon toilet for a modern one is the easiest water savings in the house. The old unit comes out in about twenty minutes. The rest is flange cleanup and shimming the new bowl level on a floor that has settled with age. We haul the old one away.

    Wall-Mounted Toilet Installation in Flint

    Wall-mounted toilets hang off a steel carrier bolted to the floor inside the wall. The tank sits behind an access plate. The carrier holds the whole load, so its feet get anchored to slab or framing and it gets plumbed before any wall finish goes on. Plan on a remodel, not a swap.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in Flint, MI

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. A flushometer needs a 1 inch supply and steady pressure. We confirm the building line can deliver the valve's rated flow, often 20 to 25 gallons a minute, before any fixture goes up. Fixture counts follow code.

    Standard Toilet Installation in Flint

    A standard floor-mount toilet is a two-hour job when the flange is sound. The stop gets shut, the tank drained, and the old bowl lifted out. After scraping the old wax off, we check the flange for cracks, set new stainless bolts, and lower the new toilet straight down onto a fresh seal so it compresses evenly. The old one leaves with us.
