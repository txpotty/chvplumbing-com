    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Flint

    Most new single-family homes in the Flint area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. We give a fixed bid per phase so you know the number before the slab is poured and do not get hit with surprise charges at trim.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling or a sewer smell usually means a vent or slope issue. We can camera the line from the cleanout to look for a belly or a bad joint while the builder warranty still applies. Construction debris is common.

    Humid summers bring condensation on cold pipes and damp basements, which can mask slow leaks. Draining outdoor spigots in the fall and keeping heat on in vacant homes stop many of the burst pipes plumbers see each winter. Pipe routes and insulation get chosen to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. The rough or top-out inspection happens before insulation and drywall, and the final comes once fixtures are set, the water heater is running and the gas is tested. Some add a gas test.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Cast iron is still used in some multifamily buildings and in walls next to bedrooms where drain noise matters, since it is much quieter than plastic when a toilet flushes upstairs.

    You can. Plenty of owners choose fixtures at a supply house showroom. We need the model numbers before rough-in, because shower valves, freestanding tub fillers and wall-mount faucets all need a specific rough-in body set at the right depth. Late changes can mean opening a wall.

    It matters more than most owners expect. Corrections need a fast return trip. An out-of-town crew might take a week to come back for one missing strap, holding up insulation and every trade behind it.
