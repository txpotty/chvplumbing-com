    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It is quick and cheap. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    Roots usually enter through an existing crack or joint, then widen it as they grow. Clay pipe can split over time. A root mass can also push a joint out of line, creating an offset that catches waste, and in bad cases the pipe collapses and has to be dug up or lined from the inside.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Bigger jobs take longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.

    Slow sinks and gurgling drains are the early signs. Sewer odor near a drain or cleanout often follows.     Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. A slow drain is the signal. Homes with root-prone sewers, old cast iron, or a kitchen that sends a lot of grease down the drain do better with a cleaning every 12 to 24 months, timed before the line slows to a crawl.
