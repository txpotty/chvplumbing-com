    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Port Huron

    Most homeowners in Port Huron, MI pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. Removing a stubborn old unit adds time. Rusted bolts get cut off, and a cast iron flange that cracks on the way out needs a repair ring, both adding labor.

    Both seal fine on a flange set at the right height. Wax has decades behind it. Foam or rubber wax-free seals forgive a toilet that has to be lifted and reset, and they do not soften the way wax can in a warm bathroom or over radiant floor heat. We pick based on your flange height.

    Start with the rough-in, since that decides which models fit at all. Next comes bowl shape. An elongated bowl is roomier but adds about two inches of length, and that matters in a tight half bath. Check the MaP flush score too.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Bigger jobs take a day or more. Wall-hung carriers and slab relocations open walls or floors, so they get booked as separate projects with cure time built in.

    We guarantee the workmanship on every toilet we set. A leak from our work gets fixed free. That covers the seal and the supply connection, while manufacturing defects in the toilet fall under the maker's warranty, which we help you file if a part fails early. Hang on to your receipt.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. Flange height and bolt torque are where it goes wrong. A flange that sits below the finished floor needs an extender or a thicker seal, and a nut tightened one turn too far can crack the base, so on new floors and wall-hung toilets a pro install is the safer bet.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. It costs more and weighs more. Two-piece toilets join tank and bowl with a gasket, so they are easier to carry upstairs and you can replace one half if it cracks. Both flush well.
