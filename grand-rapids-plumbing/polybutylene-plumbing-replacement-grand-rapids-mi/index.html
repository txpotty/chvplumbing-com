    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Grand Rapids

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. The meter often tells first. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random. Finding it lets us make a temporary repair and plan the full replacement.

    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Grand Rapids

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Start at the water heater. It also shows up under sinks, at the main and through garage walls, and we follow it into the attic to see how much of the house it feeds.

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Grand Rapids

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. On a slab home, we route the new lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, which keeps every supply line out of the concrete. PEX handles a freeze better than copper. We use expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter open, so pressure at the far shower stays strong.

    Emergency Polybutylene Pipe Repair in Grand Rapids, MI

    A burst poly fitting can push water across a floor or through a ceiling in minutes. Shut the main first. Our plumber stops the leak, replaces the failed section or fitting with a temporary repair that gets water back on and then schedules the full replacement so you are not rushed into a decision. Photos of the failure help with your insurance claim.

    Polybutylene to PEX Transitions in Grand Rapids, MI

    Sometimes the yard service line coming into the house is not polybutylene, or it was replaced already. When the service line is already sound, we tie new PEX into it at the main with a listed transition fitting and skip the trenching. The service line gets checked. If it is blue or black poly, we will recommend replacing it too.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Grand Rapids, MI

    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Labor runs lower with PEX. Attic PEX stays out of UV, clear of flues and can lights and supported as the maker specifies, which protects the pipe and the warranty.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Grand Rapids

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Grand Rapids, MI

    Some homeowners in Grand Rapids, MI want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, and join it with lead-free solder or press fittings. Copper costs more and takes longer, since each turn needs a fitting, but it tolerates heat and sunlight well and has a long track record.
