    Every Kind of Sink We Install in Grand Rapids, MI

    Kitchen Sink Installation in Grand Rapids

    Kitchen sinks come in four mounts: drop-in, undermount, farmhouse and flush-set. Each one needs its own support, and a cast iron farmhouse sink can weigh well over a hundred pounds before it holds any water, so the cabinet often needs reinforcement first. We check the cabinet before the sink arrives. Disposal and dishwasher connections are part of the job.

    Drop-In Sink Installation in Grand Rapids, MI

    Drop-in sinks are the most forgiving style. The rim hides small cutout flaws, so laminate tops take a drop-in with just a size check and the right sealant. Mounting clips snug it down. Stainless drop-ins weigh very little.

    Pedestal Sink Mounting in Grand Rapids

    The pedestal is mostly for looks. The basin itself hangs on a bracket lagged into 2x blocking behind the drywall, and if the old wall has no blocking we open it, add a board between the studs, and patch it back. Anchors in drywall will fail. A loose basin cracks the trap.

    Undermount Sink Installation in Grand Rapids

    Undermount sinks hang from the countertop. The bowl hangs on clips with a full bead of silicone, and it stays braced overnight while the adhesive cures. Glue alone is not enough. Plumbing goes on the next day.

    Undermount Clips and Cure Time in Grand Rapids

    Undermount bowls need overnight to cure. The silicone bead under the rim reaches handling strength in a few hours but needs about a day before it carries the weight of a full basin of water and a pot leaning on the edge. We brace it and come back. Plumbing goes in on the second visit.

    Double Vanity Sink Installation in Grand Rapids

    A second bowl means a second drain and two more supplies. Each bowl drains through its own trap or through a continuous waste to a shared trap, and the second faucet gets new stops. Vent sizing gets checked. Both bowls should drain without gurgling.

    Vessel Sink Installation in Grand Rapids, MI

    Vessel sinks sit on top of the counter. Since the rim sits higher, the faucet has to be taller or wall-mounted, and the drain must match whether the bowl has an overflow. No overflow means no slotted drain. Getting that wrong leaves water under the bowl.

    Commercial Restroom Sink Installation in Grand Rapids

    Public restroom lavatories take a beating. We install wall-hung and countertop units on carriers, add sensor or metering faucets, and set thermostatic mixing valves where code requires tempered water at public hand sinks. We can fit tamper-proof parts. Spec sheets go to your general contractor.
