    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Grand Rapids

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. What we will not install is an uncertified import trap, because inspectors reject them and the parts disappear after a year.

    Almost always, yes. You will usually need a plumbing permit plus a sign-off from the sewer authority's pretreatment program. Our office handles the permit paperwork and books the inspections. Expect an inspector on site.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most.     Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans.     As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap.
