    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Repair in Grand Rapids

    Most water heater repairs run between $150 and $600. Thermocouples and elements sit at the low end of that range, while gas control valves and tankless flow sensors cost noticeably more. We quote the exact price before starting.

    Rusty hot water usually means corrosion inside the tank. Either the anode rod is spent or the tank itself is starting to rust. Replacing the anode helps if the tank is sound, but a rusting lining means the end is close.

    Most repairs finish in one visit of one to two hours. If a part has to be ordered, it usually shows up in one to three business days.

    Repair usually makes sense on newer units with a single failed part. Replacement is the better call for tanks over ten to twelve years old, tanks leaking from the body or repairs that approach the cost of a new heater.

    Popping, rumbling or hissing usually means sediment on the tank bottom. Water trapped under the sediment boils and makes the noise.

    We repair Rheem, Ruud, State, Bradford White, American Standard, Navien, Rinnai and Noritz, among others. Parts that fail most on these models are stocked.

    Lukewarm water and a puddle at the base are the two signs people ignore longest. Discolored or smelly water and popping sounds also mean it is time for a look.

    Bacteria in the water react with the magnesium anode rod. A new aluminum zinc or powered anode plus a flush typically ends the smell.
