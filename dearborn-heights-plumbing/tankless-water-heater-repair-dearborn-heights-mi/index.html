    Tankless Water Heater Descaling Service in Dearborn Heights

    Scale is calcium and magnesium carbonate that drops out of hard water when it heats, and a tankless exchanger heats water fast through narrow passages. Homes around Dearborn Heights on private wells usually benefit from a water test before picking a softener, since mineral levels vary widely.     Rinnai Error Code Repair in Dearborn Heights

    Rinnai owners usually call about a code 10, which points to an air supply or exhaust blockage. We check the vent terminal for bird nests and dryer lint, then pull the front cover to inspect the intake screen and the combustion fan. A code 10 can also come from a vent run that is too long for the model. We measure.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Dearborn Heights, MI

    Rinnai units are common, and scale causes most of their service calls. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and each part gets tested against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Navien Condensate and Fan Repair in Dearborn Heights

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and scale or a backed-up condensate line is usually behind it. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. We clean the trap and screen, then retest.

    Electric Tankless Element Repair in Dearborn Heights, MI

    Electric units fail in their own way. Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart models use two to four heating elements in separate chambers, so a single burned element leaves the heater behind at high flow, while a failed flow switch means no heat at all. We check each element's resistance, measure voltage under load and check amperage on each leg, then confirm the panel and wire size match the unit's rating.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Dearborn Heights, MI

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. Our tech cleans the burner and fan, checks every vent joint, and compares the flow sensor reading to the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.
