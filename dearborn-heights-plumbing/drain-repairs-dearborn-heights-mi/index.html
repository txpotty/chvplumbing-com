    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Repairs in Dearborn Heights

    Clearing a clog usually runs $150 to $400. Pipe repairs cost more. Main line repairs can be anywhere from $800 to $5,000 or more, and excavation pushes the price toward the top of that range. The written quote comes after diagnosis.

    Age and corrosion lead the list. Soil movement is close behind. Tree roots, temperature swings, heavy loads over buried pipe, and poor original work like unprimed glue joints or missing support straps all cause early failure. Most failures have more than one cause.

    Cleaning removes a blockage from a sound pipe. Repair changes the pipe itself. A crack, a sag, a broken fitting or a missing vent will keep causing clogs no matter how often the line gets cleaned, which is why recurring clogs usually call for a fix instead of another cleaning. A camera tells you which.

    Water coming up somewhere other than where you are draining. That is the big one. If the tub fills when the washer drains, or the toilet bubbles when the sink runs, the blockage is past the point where those fixtures join, usually in the main branch or the building drain. We check it on video.

    Keep grease out of the kitchen sink. Strainers in the tub help. Flush only toilet paper, run hot water after greasy dishes, and have the main branches jetted every couple of years on older homes. Hard water homes may need it more often.

    It is high-pressure water cleaning for drain lines. Grease comes off clean. We use it for severe clogs, scale buildup and fine roots, and as a maintenance step on lines that clog often because of grease or hard water. We camera old pipe before jetting it.

    Most trap and fixture drain repairs take one to two hours. Wall work takes longer. Replacing drain pipe inside a wall or under a floor can take half a day to a full day, depending on how much finish material has to come out. The estimate includes the timeline.

    We install PVC and ABS for most new drain work. Cast iron where needed. Cast iron still makes sense in some multi-story walls where noise matters, and we tie new pipe into existing metal drains with listed transition fittings. All materials are code-listed.
