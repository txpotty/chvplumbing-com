    Irrigation Backflow Preventer Installation in Roseville

    Landscape irrigation is the most common reason homeowners need a backflow device.     Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in Roseville

    The double check valve assembly, or DCVA, is a widely used backflow preventer for low-hazard applications where the consequences of backflow are not severe. In Roseville, DCVAs are often installed on fire sprinkler systems or irrigation lines. Our certified plumbers precisely install these devices, making sure both check valves operate correctly and are properly sealed to prevent backpressure or back-siphonage into the main water supply.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Roseville

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Roseville, MI usually ranges from $400 to $1,500, depending on the type of device, its size and the complexity of the installation.     Backflow is when water flows the wrong way through a pipe and carries contaminants into the clean water supply. It can happen from back siphonage during a pressure drop or back pressure from pumps and boilers.

    It depends on the hazard and local rules. Irrigation usually uses a PVB or double check, while high hazard connections like commercial kitchens or chemical injection require an RPZ.

    CHV Plumbing stands behind our work with a full warranty on our backflow preventer installations. This warranty covers both the labor and the installed device, providing you with peace of mind. Specific warranty details will be provided with your quote.

    In Roseville, most backflow preventers must be tested annually by a certified backflow tester to confirm they are working correctly and providing adequate protection. This is a separate service from installation but is crucial for ongoing compliance and safety.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, especially if piping must be reconfigured.

    Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply.
