    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Taylor

    We offer installation for both PEX-A and PEX-B piping and understand the different advantages of each. PEX-A, often preferred for its flexibility and ability to recover from minor kinks, uses expansion fittings. PEX-B is more rigid but also very durable, typically using crimp or compression fittings. Our experts in Taylor can help you decide which type of PEX suits your project, considering factors like budget and application.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Taylor

    Copper lines can develop pinhole leaks from water chemistry, high velocity and sometimes soil contact under slabs. Well water across much of the state tends to be harder and often carries iron, which discolors sinks and clogs aerators. Once a home has had several pinhole leaks, more usually follow. Converting to PEX stops the cycle and reduces the risk of hidden water damage. We use approved transition fittings wherever new PEX connects to existing copper stubs or valves.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Taylor, MI

    Gray polybutylene pipe was common in homes built from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s and is known for sudden failures. Chlorine in municipal water breaks down the plastic and its fittings over time. Many insurers now ask about polybutylene when writing a policy. Replacing it with PEX removes that risk and can make your home easier to insure and sell.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Taylor, MI

    PEX-A, such as Uponor, uses an expansion tool to stretch the pipe and a ring over the fitting, which then shrinks back into a secure seal. The fittings have a larger inner diameter, so flow restriction is lower. PEX-A is also more flexible and can be repaired from kinks with a heat gun. It is our preferred choice for many full repipes.
