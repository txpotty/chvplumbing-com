    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Taylor

    Most new single-family homes in the Taylor area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Gas piping and tankless heaters push it up. We give a fixed bid per phase so you know the number before the slab is poured and do not get hit with surprise charges at trim.

    Some jurisdictions let owner-builders plumb a home they will live in. The work still needs permits and inspections. Inspectors hold owner-builders to the same code as licensed plumbers, and a failed underground test can cost more in delay than hiring the phase out would have cost. Many places require a license for gas.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out follows framing at three to five days, and trim waits for cabinets and counters. Trim is one or two days.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in Michigan homes. It tolerates hot water and avoids the pinhole leaks copper can develop in some water. Drains are Schedule 40 PVC. Joints under the slab are kept few, and every turn uses a long sweep so a cable can pass.

    Plumbing is usually covered under the general building permit. Where a separate plumbing permit is required, we pull it in our name as the licensed contractor. Either way the plumbing inspections and corrections are our job, and the builder never has to chase us.

    Workmanship is covered under our written warranty, and the length is spelled out in your contract. Fixtures carry their own manufacturer warranties. PEX and water heater warranties can run ten years or longer if the matching fittings are used, so we keep each system one brand.

    Water heaters work overtime through winter because incoming water is so cold, which shortens their service life. Hard freezes are routine in Michigan, and a long subzero stretch can freeze even buried water service lines in some years. Pipe routes and insulation get chosen to match. That can mean keeping supply lines out of exterior walls, insulating attic runs, and setting frost-free hose bibs that drain back inside the heated space. Attics get the most attention.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. The under-slab work runs one to three days. Top-out usually takes three to five days on a single-family house, and trim takes one or two days once cabinets, counters and tile are in place. Inspections add a day each.
