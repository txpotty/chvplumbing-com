    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Livonia

    Crawl space encapsulation involves sealing the crawl space entirely with heavy-duty vapor barriers and often includes a dehumidifier. This not only waterproofs the walls but also controls humidity, preventing mold growth and wood rot in the crawl space. Humid summers bring condensation on cold pipes and damp basements, which can mask slow leaks. An encapsulated crawl space can noticeably improve indoor air quality and energy efficiency by keeping moisture from moving up into the home. We use durable, reinforced poly sheeting for walls and floors, sealed at all seams and penetrations.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Livonia

    Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own, so water reaches the backer board in every shower. If the walls were built without a proper membrane, moisture soaks into studs and drywall. We remove failing tile, install cement board or foam board and apply a membrane such as Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban. Seams, corners and niches get extra reinforcement before new tile is set.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Livonia

    Even small cracks in your foundation walls can allow water to seep into your basement or crawl space. Our walls waterproofing service includes foundation crack repair using proven epoxy or polyurethane injection techniques. These materials fill and seal cracks, preventing future water entry and often strengthening the foundation. Addressing cracks early in Livonia homes is important to avoid larger, more costly structural issues.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Livonia, MI

    Block and stem walls often show efflorescence, which is the white mineral crust left when water passes through masonry. Irrigation and sprinklers aimed at the house are common culprits. We adjust or reroute irrigation, clean the wall and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating. Proper grading and drip line placement help keep the wall dry.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Livonia

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system typically runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, not counting new tile. Exterior block wall sealing often costs $800 to $3,000 depending on size. Below-grade foundation waterproofing generally ranges from $2,500 to $8,000.

    Skilled DIYers can roll on liquid membranes, but mistakes at seams, corners and drains often lead to leaks. Shower pans also require correct slope and flood testing. Professional installation lowers the risk of hidden water damage.

    For extensive walls waterproofing projects that involve excavation, major structural changes, or changes to drainage systems, a permit from the Livonia building department may be required. We will advise you on any required permits and help with the application process so all work complies with local codes.

    Damp proofing is a less robust solution, typically a spray-on asphalt coating that resists soil moisture but isn't designed to withstand hydrostatic pressure. Waterproofing uses stronger membranes, coatings, or drainage systems built to prevent liquid water from entering, even under pressure. For basements or crawl spaces in areas with high water tables or heavy rainfall, waterproofing is the right choice.

    Interior waterproofing addresses water after it has entered the foundation, usually managing it with sealants and drainage systems inside the basement or crawl space. Exterior waterproofing prevents water from reaching the foundation walls in the first place, usually by excavating and applying membranes or coatings to the outside of the foundation.

    We use a range of high-performance waterproofing membranes and coatings depending on the application and foundation type. These can include liquid asphalt-modified polyurethane, rubberized asphalt membranes, bentonite clay systems, and crystalline coatings. Brands often include trusted names known for their durability and effectiveness in preventing water penetration.

    When professionally installed using high-quality materials, walls waterproofing can last for decades, often 20 to 50 years or even longer. The lifespan depends on the materials used, the quality of installation, and environmental factors like soil movement and water exposure. Modern membranes and sealants are designed for lasting durability.

    Walls waterproofing is a primary step in preventing future mold growth by eliminating the moisture source. However, it does not remove existing mold. If mold is already present, it will need to be professionally remediated before or along with the waterproofing process. Once the area is dry and mold-free, waterproofing will help make sure it stays that way.
