    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Leak Repair in Dearborn

    Most wet basement repairs fall between $500 and $5,000, and the spread comes from what is causing the water. Regrading or extending downspouts is at the low end. Interior drains cost more since the slab gets cut, and exterior work costs most because of the digging. We give a written price first.

    Shut off the main water valve first, then call us. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and poorly insulated additions are the most common places for a freeze to cause a break. The split section gets cut out and replaced, then the line is pressure tested before water goes back on. Then we insulate the exposed run.

    A pipe or fitting repair usually takes an hour or two. Injecting a crack is a half-day job. Sumps take most of a day, and perimeter drains take two to five days depending on wall length.

    Tightening a loose fitting or replacing a washer hose is a reasonable DIY task. Cracked drain stacks are not. Slab leaks and sump wiring need a pro, since a mistake can flood the floor or create a shock risk.

    Gutters and downspouts do most of the work. Clear them and extend the outlets away from the walls. Inside, pour a bucket of water into the sump pit every spring to make sure the pump kicks on, check the water heater and washer hoses each year, and replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones every five years or so.

    Small repairs usually do not need a permit. Larger work is different. Replacing drain stacks, adding a sump discharge or installing a backwater valve often requires one, and we take care of the permit and the inspection so the work is documented if you sell the home later.

    Zoeller, Liberty and Wayne cover most of our installs because they are durable and parts are easy to get. We pick cast iron models for primary pumps. For homes that lose power in storms, we add a battery backup pump or a water-powered backup where city water pressure is strong enough, and we put a high-water alarm on the pit either way.

    Crack repairs use two-part polyurethane or epoxy injection resin, depending on whether the crack is actively wet. Wet cracks get polyurethane.
