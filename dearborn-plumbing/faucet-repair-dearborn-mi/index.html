    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in Dearborn

    Sputtering flow, where the water spits and surges, often means air or debris in the line after a water main repair, or a partly clogged cartridge screen. Flushing usually clears it. On pull-down faucets, the check valves in the hose can stick too, and we clean or replace those.

    Dripping Faucet Solutions in Dearborn

    Every drip has a source, and on a faucet that has dripped for years there is usually more than one worn part. We replace what failed and check the rest. Each valve type, from compression to ceramic disc, wears in its own way, so the repair is matched to the valve inside yours.

    Outdoor Hose Bib Repair in Dearborn

    Outdoor spigots take a beating from sun and weather. Hard freezes are routine in Michigan, and a long subzero stretch can freeze even buried water service lines in some years. Packing and seat washers get replaced on standard hose bibs, and we install frost-free sillcocks with the valve seat inside the warm wall when an old one has split. Every new spigot gets a vacuum breaker for backflow protection.

    Disc Faucet Repair in Dearborn

    Ceramic disc faucets last a long time, but grit from a water main repair or scale from hard water can scratch the discs or wear out the rubber inlet seals underneath. Inlet seals cost very little. A scratched disc means a new cartridge, since the two discs are lapped to fit each other and cannot be resurfaced.

    Faucet Repair Versus Replacement in Dearborn, MI

    A new cartridge usually costs well under the price of a new faucet and can add years of life, as long as the faucet body is sound and parts are still made. A cracked body or discontinued parts change the answer. We give you both prices so the choice is yours.

    Leaking Faucet Handle and Base Repair in Dearborn, MI

    Water seeping around the handle or pooling at the base usually means failed O-rings on the spout or cartridge, or a mounting nut that has worked loose under the sink. Check the cabinet floor. New O-rings go on and the mounting nut gets tightened, then reseal the base so water on the counter cannot get under it.

    Hard Water Faucet Repair in Dearborn

    Scale is the biggest reason faucet seals wear early, because the mineral crust scratches rubber and keeps ceramic discs from closing flat. Hard water speeds it up. The valve body gets cleaned while the old part is out, so the new cartridge or washer seals against a smooth surface.

    Pull-Down Kitchen Faucet Repair in Dearborn

    Pull-down kitchen faucets wear at the hose, the spray head and the docking magnet or counterweight that pulls the hose back into the spout. The hose usually splits near the quick-connect fitting. We replace hoses, spray heads and weights with brand parts, then check for drips under the sink where the hose connects.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Dearborn

    Most faucet repairs in Dearborn run $120 to $350, depending on the valve type and the parts, with a washer job at the low end and a high-end cartridge at the top. Touchless faucets can cost more. You get a written price before any work.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If we have to order a part, there may be a quick second trip.

    On an older compression faucet, a washer swap is a fair DIY job if you can find the right washer and the seat underneath is smooth. Cartridge faucets are trickier. Forcing a stuck cartridge can crack the valve body, which turns a repair into a new faucet.

    A drip from the spout, water around the handle or base, a handle that is stiff or loose, and a temperature that drifts while you use it are the usual signs. Noises like squealing or banging count as well. So does weak flow from just one faucet.

    Over time, yes. Mineral deposits wear cartridges, block aerators and dull the finish, and a water softener plus regular aerator cleaning can add years to a faucet in a hard water home. A vinegar soak loosens aerator scale.

    Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard and Pfister cover most of what we see in Michigan homes, and we stock their common cartridges. Other brands are fine too.

    Repair means replacing worn parts inside the faucet you have, like washers, seats, O-rings or a cartridge, while replacement means a whole new faucet on the sink. Fixing it usually costs less. A cracked or discontinued faucet is better replaced.

    Low pressure at a single faucet is almost always the aerator or the cartridge. If it is low everywhere, look at the pressure regulator, the supply or aging pipe.
