    Under-Slab Sewer Replacement in Dearborn

    Sewer pipe under a slab is the hardest kind to reach. We have two ways in.     Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Dearborn, MI

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. That is a lot of joints for roots to find. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall softens and deforms under the weight of the soil, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Dearborn, MI

    Trenching lets us fix grade. That is its big advantage. When an old line has a sag that holds water and solids, bursting would just follow the same sag, so we dig and reset the pipe on a proper bed at a proper fall. Bellies disappear.

    Sewer Scope Inspections for Replacement in Dearborn

    A sewer camera on a push cable goes in through the cleanout. The footage shows each joint, crack and root intrusion. A small transmitter called a sonde sits behind the camera head, so we can find its exact spot and depth from the yard above, which keeps the digging small. Bring questions.

    Trenchless Sewer Repair Options in Dearborn, MI

    Pipe bursting needs two pits. We dig a launch pit near the house and a receiving pit near the main. A cone-shaped head is pulled through the old line, breaking it outward, while fused HDPE pipe follows right behind it, which puts new pipe in the same spot without digging the yard. Driveways stay put.

    Sewer Pipe Material Upgrades in Dearborn, MI

    Original sewer pipe in older homes is usually clay, cast iron or Orangeburg. Clay joints open up and let roots in, cast iron scales and cracks from the inside, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid 1900s, squashes into an oval over time. Replacement means PVC or HDPE.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Dearborn

    Digging is still the right method for a lot of lines. If the old pipe has caved in, trenchless has nothing to follow. Sharp bends, a belly that needs regrading, or a route that should change all point toward an open trench, and we restore the sod or gravel afterward. Grass grows back.

    Diagnosis comes before price. Video tells us the problem and the sonde tells us the spot. We sometimes add a hydro-jet cleaning first, because a line full of grease can hide a crack, then camera again so we are looking at the pipe itself. Guesswork costs money.
