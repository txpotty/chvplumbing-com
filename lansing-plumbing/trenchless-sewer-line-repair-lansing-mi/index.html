    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Lansing

    Cured-in-place lining creates a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line, then pull or invert a resin-saturated liner into place and cure it with hot water, steam or UV light. The result is a seamless, jointless pipe that roots cannot penetrate. Lining works well for cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their shape.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Lansing, MI

    CIPP relining creates a new pipe within an existing damaged sewer line. A resin-saturated felt tube is inserted into the old pipe and then inflated, curing in place to form a smooth, durable and watertight liner. This technique is excellent for repairing cracks, leaks and minor offsets without needing to dig up the entire line. It is a quick and effective solution for many common sewer problems faced by Michigan homeowners.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Lansing

    Some older homes still have Orangeburg fiber pipe or vitrified clay sewer laterals. Orangeburg flattens and blisters over time, while clay cracks and separates at joints. Pipe bursting is often the best option for Orangeburg, and lining can work well for clay that still holds its shape. We determine the material on camera before recommending a method.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Lansing, MI

    Cast iron drains and sewer lines corrode from the inside, leaving rough scale, cracks and channel rot along the bottom of the pipe. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or cutters, then line it to restore a smooth, sealed interior. This is especially useful for cast iron running under slabs where excavation is costly. Lining can add many years of service.
