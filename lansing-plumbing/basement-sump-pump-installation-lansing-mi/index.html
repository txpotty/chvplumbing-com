    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Lansing

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a good fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we generally recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod adjusted to your pit depth.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Lansing

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, built to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    Heavy summer rainstorms in Michigan can overwhelm combined and storm sewers and push water back up through basement floor drains. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is strongly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained once a year, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and making sure the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and extends the life of your pump.

    In many areas, including Lansing, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to ensure compliance with local building codes. CHV Plumbing will help you navigate the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching typically takes most of a day.

    It is recommended to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to confirm the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps catch issues early.

    Replacing a pump generally does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they apply.
