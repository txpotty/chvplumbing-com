    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Kalamazoo

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Our installs carry a written warranty on both parts and labor. You will see the exact length in your service agreement, and the pump manufacturer adds its own coverage, which on a quality cast iron pump usually runs three to five years from the date of purchase. We handle the registration.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. That is the whole test.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. New pits, new outlets and discharge routing are a different story, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in Kalamazoo. Hire a licensed plumber for those.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Other signs are rust on the housing and rapid cycling, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. After about ten years, plan on replacing it.
