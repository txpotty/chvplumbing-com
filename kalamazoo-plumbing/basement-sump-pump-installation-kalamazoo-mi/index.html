    CHV Plumbing offers a full warranty on our basement sump pump installations, covering both parts and labor. The specific warranty period will be detailed in your service agreement. We stand behind our work and the quality of the products we install in your Kalamazoo home.

    Many homes in Michigan were built before the 1970s and still have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains. Homes built on slabs without basements seldom need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water fast during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    Spring snowmelt and high groundwater keep sump pumps running hard, so a backup pump or battery system is worth considering. Because storms and power outages tend to arrive together, a battery backup sump pump is highly recommended. It provides crucial protection by switching on automatically when the main power fails, so your basement stays dry even without electricity. It is a wise investment for peace of mind.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed by local codes.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also wise to consider a replacement before it fails completely.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, designed to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained once a year, ideally before the rainy season.
