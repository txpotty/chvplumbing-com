    Understanding Polybutylene Failure in Kalamazoo

    Polybutylene's inherent weakness stems from its chemical reaction with oxidants like chlorine, commonly used in municipal water treatment. Over time, this reaction causes the pipes and their plastic fittings to become brittle, leading to hairline cracks and eventual rupture. Well water across much of the state tends to be harder and often carries iron, which stains sinks and clogs aerators. Hard water can also add stress to pipe that is already weakened.     Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Kalamazoo

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines typically add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. Homes built from the late 1970s to mid 1990s are most likely to have it.

    Polybutylene plumbing replacement is a complex job that requires specialized tools, knowledge of plumbing codes, and experience with various piping materials and connection methods. Attempting a DIY replacement can lead to costly errors, future leaks, and may not pass inspections. It's best left to a licensed plumber.

    Properly installed PEX piping usually has a lifespan of 25 to 50 years, while copper piping can last 50 to 70 years or even longer. Both are major upgrades from polybutylene and offer decades of reliable service for your Kalamazoo, MI home.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Fittings and hot water lines often fail first.

    We primarily use quality, trusted brands for PEX such as Uponor (formerly Wirsbo), Viega, and Zurn, known for their durability and performance. For copper, we use standard Type L or Type M copper tubing from reputable manufacturers, providing long-lasting reliability.

    Both PEX and copper are solid choices. PEX is more flexible, resistant to freezing, and generally more affordable for installation. Copper is very durable, lasts a very long time, and is resistant to UV light. We'll help you choose the best option for your Michigan home and budget.
