    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Novi

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Start at the water heater. You may also see it under sinks, near the main shutoff or coming through the garage wall, and we trace runs through the attic to confirm how much of the house it serves.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Novi, MI

    Some homeowners in Novi, MI want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. Our copper replacements use thicker Type L pipe with lead-free soldered or pressed joints, not the thinner Type M. Expect a higher price and a slightly longer job with copper, in exchange for good heat and UV tolerance and a long history.

    Whole-Home PEX Replacement in Novi, MI

    PEX-A is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, scale resistant and easy to route. We use Uponor PEX-A with expansion fittings that do not restrict flow. Every fixture, appliance and the water heater gets new pipe from the main, and most houses are finished in two to four days with water on each evening.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Novi

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and faucets. Hot water speeds it up. Hot lines and fittings near the water heater fail more than anything else.

    Polybutylene Replacement Costs in Novi, MI

    Replacing polybutylene with PEX in an average home often costs $5,000 to $12,000, depending on size and bathrooms. Copper runs higher.     Copper Replacement Option in Novi

    Type L copper appeals to some homeowners for its long history and rigid feel. We solder or press each joint. Since copper is pricier and slower to install, we walk through cost, water conditions and your plans for the house before you decide.

    A full replacement takes every poly supply line out of service, not just the sections that have failed. Leaving some poly keeps the risk. New pipe goes from the main to every fixture and appliance, each one gets a new quarter-turn stop and the system is tested before any wall is closed.
