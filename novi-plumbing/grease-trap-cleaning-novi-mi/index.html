    Why Choose CHV Plumbing for Grease Trap Cleaning in Novi, MI?

    A grease trap works by slowing the water down long enough for fat to float and food to sink. Past a quarter full, that stops. The grease then slips past the outlet into the lateral, cools into a hard white crust, and a cable machine only bores a narrow hole through it.

    • Prevent Blockages, Regular pumping keeps floor sinks and floor drains flowing during the dinner rush.
    • Prevent Backups, Pumping on schedule keeps grease from leaving the trap and hardening in the lateral.
    • Complete Pump-Outs, We pull grease, water and the solids on the floor every time.

    Citywide Coverage

    School cafeterias and church kitchens around Novi usually have small traps that get forgotten for a year at a time. Those go on a schedule as well. For a kitchen that serves lunch on weekdays and shuts down for summer, pumping once a quarter is usually plenty.

    Experienced Team

    Techs ask the kitchen staff questions, because the cook knows more about what goes down the drain than anyone. Habits change grease loads. Scraping plates into the trash before they hit the dish machine can stretch a pumping interval by weeks, and our techs will point that out when they see it.

    Safety-First Approach

    We do not sell enzyme or bacteria additives as a replacement for pumping. Many programs do not allow it. The additives split grease into fine droplets that slip past the outlet and harden again downstream, the very thing the trap is there to prevent.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Novi

    Most visits in Novi run somewhere between $200 and $800, set mostly by trap size and how long it has gone since the last pump-out. Hard access adds time. A trap behind a walk-in that needs 80 feet of hose costs more than one sitting next to the curb. You get the price first.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units. Those AGRUs have a heater and a skimmer wheel that pull grease into a container, and they need the container emptied and the wheel and screen cleaned on a schedule.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Plenty of busy restaurants pump monthly. A coffee shop with a light menu may go two or three months, provided the depth readings stay below 25 percent.

    Two things, a full pump-out and a trap that drains properly when we leave. We do not skim and call it done. If a drain problem traced to our visit shows up afterward, we send the crew back to fix it, and the depth readings on the sheet show what the trap looked like when we finished.

    Staff can clean a small under-sink trap between pump-outs. The waste still needs legal disposal. Many kitchens have staff skim daily and book us to fully pump and scrape every few weeks, which keeps the interval long without skipping the records the inspector expects.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. The disposal is the part that gets people fined. Pretreatment programs often require a licensed hauler's record for each pump-out, so a kitchen that handles its own cleaning may have no paperwork to show when the inspector asks.

    Under-sink traps take 30 to 60 minutes. Plan one to three hours for an outdoor interceptor. Big tanks with a hardened cap take the longest, since the cap has to be broken up with a pressure wand before the hose will pull it.

    Short term, slow drains and smell. Longer term, a hardened grease cap that can take hours to break up. Hydrogen sulfide from the rotting grease also attacks concrete, so interceptors that go unpumped for years often need new baffles and risers well before their time.
