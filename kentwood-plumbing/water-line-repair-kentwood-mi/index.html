    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Kentwood

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Kentwood

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older neighborhoods may also have lead service lines, which the state is requiring utilities to replace over time. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. We price each one for you.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Kentwood

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. It works best on straight runs. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, while new HDPE comes through right behind the head. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Kentwood, MI

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. Shutting the water off comes first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Kentwood, MI

    Water Pressure Restoration in Kentwood

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the main shutoff and the curb valve for anything half closed, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Kentwood, MI

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Most homes are done in a day.
