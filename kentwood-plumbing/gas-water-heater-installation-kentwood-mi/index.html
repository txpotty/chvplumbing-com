    Proper Sizing and Venting in Kentwood, MI

    Sizing and venting decide whether a gas heater runs safe and efficient. We size the heater from your peak use, then match the vent to the manufacturer table and code. Undersized vents spill CO. We test it every time.

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Installation in Kentwood, MI

    Rheem gas heaters show up in a lot of local homes, from basic atmospheric tanks to power vent models. CHV Plumbing installs the full Rheem line. We connect the gas, set the flue and check the Guardian flammable vapor sensor and the combustion air path so the safety features work as designed. Then we test draft.

    High-Efficiency Gas Water Heater Installation in Kentwood, MI

    High-efficiency gas heaters use condensing technology to pull extra heat out of the exhaust. Because the exhaust comes out cool and wet, they vent through PVC and need a condensate drain, which changes where the heater can go and what the install involves. Energy Star models qualify for some rebates. We install Rheem, Bradford White and AO Smith models.

    Tankless Gas Water Heater Conversion in Kentwood

    Converting from a tank to tankless is usually more than a swap. A conversion often means a larger gas line, new venting, a condensate drain and service valves. It takes most of a day. We do all of it to code.

    Cost-Effective Gas Water Heater Upgrades in Kentwood, MI

    Upgrading an old 0.55 UEF gas tank to a newer model can cut gas use noticeably. You get a side-by-side of efficiency, recovery and estimated yearly cost before you choose. Payback varies by use. We also explain basic upkeep.

    Power Vent Gas Water Heater Installation in Kentwood, MI

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust through PVC or other approved pipe. That means the heater can go in a spot with no chimney or vertical flue, and the vent can run sideways out an exterior wall as long as it stays within the length limit. A 120-volt outlet is required. We terminate the vent to spec.

    Traditional Tank Gas Water Heater Installation in Kentwood

    Tank gas heaters are still the workhorse in most garages and utility rooms, usually 40 or 50 gallons. Each install gets a new gas connector and drip leg, a vent connector pitched upward and a relief line piped where code allows. Strapping where required. Sizing follows household demand.
