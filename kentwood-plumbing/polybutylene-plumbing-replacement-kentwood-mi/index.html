    Why Polybutylene Fails in Kentwood

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Permits for Polybutylene Replacement in Kentwood

    A whole-house poly replacement in Kentwood is a repipe, and it usually needs a plumbing permit. We file it. The inspector looks at the new lines with the walls still open, checking supports, nail plates, shutoffs and the pressure test before anything is patched.     Copper Pipe Replacement in Kentwood

    Copper is a solid alternative to PEX for poly replacement. It is rigid, handles heat well and has decades of history in homes. Plan on a higher price. Some water can pinhole copper over time, so we look at your water and any past leak history before recommending it over PEX.
