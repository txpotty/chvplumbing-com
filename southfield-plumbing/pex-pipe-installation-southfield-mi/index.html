    Every PEX job gets a pressure test before the walls close. The gauge tells the story. A crooked crimp or a half-seated expansion fitting shows up on the test while it is still easy to reach, not months later behind fresh paint.

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Southfield

    Low pressure at every faucet, not just one, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures.     Hydronic Heating with PEX in Southfield

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating loops need oxygen-barrier PEX, which has a coating that keeps oxygen from migrating through the wall and rusting the boiler, the pump and any iron in the system. Loop length matters as well. Run them too long and the last stretch never warms up.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Southfield, MI

    A single bad section is not always a reason to repipe. We can cut out a split or corroded stretch of copper, transition to PEX with a listed fitting, and have the water back on in an hour or two.     PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Southfield, MI

    Price on a repipe comes down to a few things: bathroom count, square footage, how many stories, and how easy the attic and walls are to work in. PEX almost always comes in below copper because the material is cheaper and a crew can run a house in two to four days instead of a week. Patching is sometimes quoted on its own line. We price PEX-A, PEX-B and copper side by side when you ask.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Southfield

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A makes tighter bends, can be heat-fixed if it kinks, and its fittings flow better, while PEX-B is cheaper and its crimps hold just as well when each one is gauged. Both last decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.
