    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Southfield

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Start at the water heater. It also shows up under sinks, at the main and through garage walls, and we follow it into the attic to see how much of the house it feeds.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Southfield, MI

    Some homeowners in Southfield, MI want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. Our copper replacements use thicker Type L pipe with lead-free soldered or pressed joints, not the thinner Type M. Copper costs more and takes longer, since each turn needs a fitting, but it tolerates heat and sunlight well and has a long track record.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Southfield

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and faucets. Hot water speeds it up. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.

    Partial Polybutylene Replacement in Southfield, MI

    Full replacement is the right long-term fix, but it is not always possible right away. Sometimes it has to wait. We can replace the highest-risk sections first, like the lines and fittings near the water heater and in the attic, and plan the rest for later so you are not carrying all of the risk in the meantime. We will be honest about what is left.

    Whole-Home PEX Replacement in Southfield, MI

    PEX-A is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, scale resistant and easy to route. We use Uponor PEX-A with expansion fittings that do not restrict flow. New lines run from the main shutoff to every fixture, water heater and appliance, and most homes are completed in two to four working days with water back on each night.

    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Southfield

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. The meter often tells first. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random. Finding it lets us make a temporary repair and plan the full replacement.

    Fitting Failure and Emergency Repair in Southfield

    Acetal plastic and copper crimp ring fittings are often the first part of a poly system to fail. A burst fitting in an attic or wall can cause major damage in minutes. After an emergency repair we schedule the full replacement, since removing the poly system is the only lasting fix.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Southfield, MI

    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Faster install means lower labor cost. Attic PEX stays out of UV, clear of flues and can lights and supported as the maker specifies, which protects the pipe and the warranty.
