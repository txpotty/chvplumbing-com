    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Southfield, MI

    Power outages are common, especially during severe weather, and they leave your primary sump pump useless.     Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Southfield

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which protects the motor. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and unheated additions are the most common places for a freeze to cause a break.     Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Southfield

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    CHV Plumbing offers a full warranty on our basement sump pump installations, covering both parts and labor. The specific warranty period will be spelled out in your service agreement. We stand behind our work and the quality of the products we install in your Southfield home.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching usually takes most of a day.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less noticeable. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and usually more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    Manufacturer warranties typically range from 3 to 5 years on quality cast iron pumps. We also back our installation labor, and we will walk you through the specific coverage for the model you choose.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed by local codes.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it must be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.
