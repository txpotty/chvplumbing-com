    Preventive Hydrojet Maintenance in Southfield

    Preventive jetting pays off for lines that clog more than once a year. We set the interval from camera findings. A line with heavy grease might need jetting every six months, while a sewer with a few root entry points might only need it every 18 to 24 months, and the footage tells us which.

    Chemical-Free Drain Cleaning in Southfield, MI

    Hydrojetting uses only water, so nothing caustic goes into your pipes or septic tank. Caustic cleaners can harm old pipe. Jetting removes the buildup physically, and the debris flushes to the sewer or septic tank as normal waste, so there is nothing left in the pipe to eat at joints or harm the bacteria a septic system depends on.

    Whole-House Drain Jetting in Southfield

    Slow drains in several rooms point to buildup in the main or a branch line. We jet the main from the cleanout and the branches from roof vents or from fixture access points as needed for that layout. Most homes take two to three hours.

    Main Line Jetting in Southfield, MI

    The lateral from your house to the street handles every drain in the house, so buildup there causes backups everywhere at once. We jet it from the cleanout to the tap. Low spots where the pipe has settled hold water and collect sludge, so we spend extra passes there and mark the location on the camera footage in case a repair makes sense later.

    Emergency Hydrojet Services in Southfield, MI

    A full sewer backup cannot wait for a weekday slot. First, we open the line. A cable opens it, then the jetter removes the grease, roots or wipes that caused it.

    Hydrojetting vs. Snaking in Southfield, MI

    Snaking pokes a hole through a clog. Jetting cleans the whole pipe. Snaking suits a one-time clog, while jetting fixes a line that clogs repeatedly.

    Storm and Yard Drain Jetting in Southfield, MI

    Yard drains, channel drains and downspout lines fill with sand, silt and leaves between storms. Backwater valves and well-maintained sump pumps are among the most effective defenses against flooded basements. A clogged yard drain sends that water back toward the house, so we jet the lines from the outlet end and flush the catch basins before the wet season starts. Pop-up emitters get checked too.
