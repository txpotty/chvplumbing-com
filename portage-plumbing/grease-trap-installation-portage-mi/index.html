    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Portage

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. We size the trap to that peak flow, not the average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. We set the unit, run the inlet and outlet in schedule 40 PVC or cast iron, and add the flow control fitting the manufacturer calls for.

    Grease Trap Installation Cost in Portage, MI

    Price follows size and location more than brand. The low end is a small indoor unit with easy access and a short pipe run. The high end is an outdoor gravity interceptor with excavation, a traffic-rated lid and a permit, which can run five figures once concrete restoration is counted. We quote after a site visit.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Portage

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. Before anything ships, we check that the tank and covers carry an H-20 traffic rating where trucks will roll over them. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Portage

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Portage

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. We work from the architect's plumbing sheets and the kitchen equipment schedule. Changes get expensive. Moving a trap after the concrete cures means cutting the floor, so we catch problems while it is still paper.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Portage

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. They cost more up front. They also need a dedicated electrical circuit, which we coordinate with your electrician before the unit arrives. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Portage

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. A 50 GPM trap on a line that sees 75 GPM will pass grease every lunch rush. We do the math on paper. Each sink compartment's volume, the drain size and a one or two minute drain time give us the GPM figure the code uses.

    Food Truck Commissary and Ghost Kitchen Traps in Portage, MI

    Commissary kitchens see a dozen trucks dumping wash water in the same two hours, which is a different load from a restaurant spreading it over a long shift. We size for that spike. Ghost kitchens with four or five brands under one roof often share a single trap, so the fixture count can be higher than the floor plan suggests. Each brand's prep sink counts.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Portage

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Outdoor tanks cost more, mostly for digging and putting the site back. Brand is a small part of it. We give you a written estimate after we walk the site, and it does not change unless we hit something underground nobody could see.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We measure each connected fixture and calculate the gallons per minute they can send at once. Gravity tanks use volume instead. For those, the local pretreatment program often has its own formula based on seats or fixture units, and we use theirs.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Inside the trap, warm wastewater slows and cools, so grease rises and stays put while water flows on. Grease that reaches the mains hardens into blockages, and the sewer authority bills the kitchens it traces back to. Your own drain lines benefit too.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. Concrete tanks can last decades, though hydrogen sulfide gas slowly eats the concrete above the water line. Cleaning habits matter most. Rough cleaning and long gaps between pump-outs shorten the life of every type.
