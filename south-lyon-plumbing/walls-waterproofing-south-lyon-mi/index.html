    Look for bubbling paint, soft drywall, stained baseboards and musty odors. White crust on block or stucco walls is another sign of moisture moving through. Loose tiles or cracked grout in a shower also mean water is getting behind.

    Sprinklers and drip lines aimed at the house are the most common cause. Heavy storms and poor grading also send water toward walls. Masonry absorbs that water and leaves mineral deposits as it dries.

    When professionally installed using high-quality materials, walls waterproofing can last for decades, often 20 to 50 years or even longer. The lifespan depends on the materials used, the quality of installation, and environmental factors like soil movement and water exposure. Modern membranes and sealants are designed for lasting durability.

    A shower wall waterproofing project usually takes two to four days, including drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing can often be completed in a day. Larger foundation jobs may take a few days.

    Look for signs such as damp or wet spots on basement or crawl space walls, a musty odor, visible mold or mildew growth, efflorescence, a white powdery residue, on concrete or brick, and standing water.     Damp proofing is a less durable solution, typically a spray-on asphalt coating that resists soil moisture but isn't designed to withstand hydrostatic pressure. Waterproofing uses stronger membranes, coatings, or drainage systems specifically engineered to prevent liquid water from entering, even under pressure.
