    Type K copper is the thick-walled grade made for burial.     Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Installation in South Lyon

    Residential water lines usually run $1,800 to $5,500 depending on length, soil and material. Rock, boring or copper can raise the total to $6,000 to $10,000 or more.

    Soils in Michigan range from heavy clay to sandy ground, and many areas have a high water table that keeps basements damp. Expansive clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, which pushes and pulls on buried pipe. Over years that movement can crack rigid pipe or pull a fitting apart, so we favor flexible PEX or HDPE, sand bedding and a little slack in the line where the ground moves most in South Lyon. Flexible pipe forgives movement.

    Depth rules depend on your jurisdiction and climate. Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and unheated additions are the most common places for a freeze to cause a break. Lines have to sit below the local frost line, so colder places need deeper trenches than mild ones.

    Plan on one to three days for most residential mains in South Lyon. How long the run is and how hard the digging goes set the pace, along with weather and how quickly the permit office schedules the inspection that has to happen before the trench gets backfilled. Rock slows everything.

    This is a job for a licensed plumber. You are dealing with excavation, pipe sizing and code rules, plus coordination with the water utility at the meter, and a poor install can lead to leaks, water damage and contamination of your drinking water. The risk is not worth it.

    Uponor Wirsbo and Viega PureFlow cover most of our PEX work. For copper we use Type L inside and Type K for burial, sourced from manufacturers that meet ASTM standards, so the pipe holds up in the conditions it will actually see. Wall thickness matters in the ground.
