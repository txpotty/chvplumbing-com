    Toilet Brands We Service in Farmington Hills

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber and Mansfield make up most of the toilets we open, and each one uses its own flapper size or flush valve design. Put the wrong flapper on one and it will leak within weeks. We read the model number cast inside the tank wall and stock the common ones on every truck.

    Sensor and Flushometer Repair in Farmington Hills

    Sensor flush valves fail in their own ways, from dead batteries and dirty sensor eyes to solenoids that stick open and keep a bowl running. We test the sensor before we replace anything. Batteries are the cheap fix. If the valve flushes on its own, the sensor range or a dirty lens is usually to blame.

    Older Toilet Brands We Service in Farmington Hills, MI

    Older toilets from Eljer, Briggs, Crane and Universal Rundle still turn up in homes built before the 1990s, and their flush valves often take parts nobody stocks anymore. Some can take an adapter flapper, and we carry a few kinds. For the rest, we will tell you if parts are available or if replacement is the honest answer.

    Leaking Toilet Repair in Farmington Hills

    Water on the floor can come from four places: the wax ring, the tank-to-bowl gasket, the supply line or sweat running off a cold tank. Each one needs a different fix. We dry everything and watch where the water shows up first, which takes about ten minutes and saves pulling a toilet that did not need it. A cracked bowl gets a replacement quote, not a patch.

    Flush Mechanism Repair in Farmington Hills, MI

    A weak flush usually means the tank is not filling to the fill line, the chain has too much slack, or the flapper is closing before the tank empties. We bring the water up to the line stamped on the overflow tube and adjust the chain to about half an inch of slack. Handles break too. A loose or cracked handle is a five-minute swap.

    Clogged Toilet Clearing in Farmington Hills, MI

    Most clogs sit in the trapway, the curved channel cast into the porcelain, and a closet auger with a rubber-sleeved head clears them without scratching the bowl. Plungers miss some. When the auger finds nothing and the bowl still fills, the blockage is further down the drain or in the main line. At that point we run a cable from the nearest cleanout or send a camera down to see it.

    Toilet Leaking Through the Ceiling in Farmington Hills, MI

    Water coming through the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom usually traces back to a failed wax ring or a cracked supply nut, and the drywall below holds the evidence. Stop using that toilet.
