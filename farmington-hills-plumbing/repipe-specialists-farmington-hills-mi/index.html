    Slab Leak Repiping in Farmington Hills

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Basements are the norm, so sump pumps, drain tile and foundation drainage are a big part of residential plumbing. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.

    Condo, Townhome and Multi-Family Repipes in Farmington Hills

    Shared walls and HOA rules make repiping attached homes more involved. We coordinate with the HOA, schedule water shutoffs that may affect neighbors and protect adjoining units from dust and noise as much as we can. Property managers get phased schedules. Big buildings get repiped by stack or wing, which lets residents stay home.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Farmington Hills

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. Our copper repipes use Type L with proper hangers and isolators so the pipe does not wear against framing. Lead-free solder only. Copper can pinhole in aggressive water, so we ask about your water and any past leaks before recommending it over PEX.

    Drywall Patching and Restoration in Farmington Hills, MI

    Repiping needs small openings in walls and ceilings to reach fixtures and pull pipe. We cut clean, square openings, protect the area around them and patch every hole once the plumbing passes its test. Textures get matched. We match knockdown, orange peel or smooth walls, so the patches are ready to prime and paint.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Farmington Hills, MI

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Farmington Hills

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. Our crews use Uponor or similar PEX-A, where the pipe end gets expanded and shrinks back over the fitting for a full-diameter connection. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Farmington Hills, MI

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    PEX Repiping in Farmington Hills, MI

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. It resists scale and chlorine, handles a freeze better than copper and costs less to install, which is why most of our repipes use it. Our PEX-A installs use expansion fittings that do not shrink the flow path. That keeps pressure up at the far fixtures.
