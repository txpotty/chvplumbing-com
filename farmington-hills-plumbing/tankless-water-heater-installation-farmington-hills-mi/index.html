    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Farmington Hills, MI

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Farmington Hills, MI homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. We check the panel first.

    Cost-Effective Tankless Conversions in Farmington Hills, MI

    A tankless conversion in Farmington Hills, MI costs more than a tank swap, mostly because of the gas line upgrade, the new venting and the unit price itself. The savings come back over time. With no standby loss and a lifespan that can reach 20 years, many homeowners see the difference on the gas bill within the first few winters. We give you real numbers.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Farmington Hills

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. The built-in pump can move hot water toward a distant bath on a timer, cutting the wait at the tap without a separate pump. Navien vents in PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Farmington Hills

    Upgrading from a tank in Farmington Hills begins with your current setup: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits. In some homes the old flue gets capped and the new unit vents out a side wall in PVC, which is simpler and opens the old chase. Other homes need a longer run. We spell that out on the quote.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Farmington Hills

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Farmington Hills, MI

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. The vent changes as well. Both are on the estimate.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Farmington Hills, MI

    Replacing an old tankless unit is usually simpler than a first-time conversion. The gas line is already sized and the vent path already exists, so in many homes it is a matter of matching the connections, checking the vent for damage and hanging the new unit. Vent sizes can change by brand. We check before ordering.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Farmington Hills

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job because the old tank spot is not always the best place. Mounting near the gas meter on a garage or outside wall shortens the gas line and venting, though it can add distance to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. A far bath may need recirc.
