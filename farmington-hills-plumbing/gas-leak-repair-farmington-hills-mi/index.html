    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Farmington Hills

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. We read the tag first, since it lists what the utility found. Then we test everything.     Gas Line Repair and Replacement in Farmington Hills, MI

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. We take the joint apart, clean the threads and remake it with fresh gas-rated sealant. Cracked fittings get replaced. Badly rusted sections, often near a crawlspace vent, are cut back to clean pipe and replaced. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a gauge test.

    Permitting and Code Compliance in Farmington Hills, MI

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. You will know before we start whether a permit applies. The fee goes on the estimate. The work follows Michigan adopted fuel gas code and any local amendments.     Appliance Gas Line Repair in Farmington Hills, MI

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is lit again.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Farmington Hills, MI

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with underground tanks get a line test too.

    Permit and Inspection Coordination in Farmington Hills

    Replacing more than a connector usually means a permit, and in Farmington Hills the inspector will want to see a gauge holding pressure before the pipe is covered. The permit and the inspection booking are on us. Most inspections take under twenty minutes.     Appliance Connector Replacement in Farmington Hills

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. Any of those still in service should be replaced. We see them weekly. The replacement is a coated stainless connector sized for the appliance, with a new ball valve and a sediment trap where one is required. Bubble testing follows before the burner is lit.
