    Main Water Line Leak Detection in Sterling Heights

    A soft wet patch near the sidewalk or a sinking spot over the line can mean the service pipe is leaking. Old galvanized and copper lines fail most. We map the pipe with a line tracer and listen for the leak with a ground microphone, which keeps the dig to a single hole rather than a trench. Then we patch or replace.

    Hidden Wall and Ceiling Leaks in Sterling Heights

    Bubbling paint, soft drywall and a musty smell come up on most of our calls. All of them mean water. A thermal scan shows the wet patch, and a moisture meter confirms it. Acoustic tools help when the leak is on a pressurized line, and once the source is pinned down we cut a single neat access opening instead of chasing the stain across the wall.

    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Sterling Heights, MI

    The buried line from your meter to the house can leak for months without surfacing, especially in old galvanized pipe. Green grass in a dry spell is a clue. We trace the line with an electronic locator, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak, switching to tracer gas on plastic lines that do not carry sound.

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Sterling Heights, MI

    On a ground microphone, copper leaks are easy to hear. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. Both show up on our schedule every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX top the list of what we find.

    Commercial Leak Detection in Sterling Heights

    In Sterling Heights, a commercial building has long supply runs where a leak can hide behind finished walls for a long time. Zones get tested one at a time. Our techs isolate risers, mains and fire lines with pressure gauges, then locate leaks with acoustic and thermal tools, working after hours when tenants or customers would otherwise be in the way.

    Slab Leak Detection in Sterling Heights

    Concrete slab homes have supply lines buried in sand below the floor, and a pinhole there can run for months before anyone notices. A warm tile is the classic sign. We pressure test the hot and cold sides separately, then listen through the slab with a ground microphone and confirm with a thermal camera on hot water lines.

    Pool Leak Detection in Sterling Heights

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation probably has a leak. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, listen for leaks with a hydrophone underwater, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain so we can point to the exact fitting or crack.

    Sewer Line Leak Detection in Sterling Heights, MI

    A cracked drain line leaks wastewater into the soil, which can show up as a sewer smell in the yard, a soggy patch or slow drains. We use a camera for these. The camera goes in at the cleanout and runs to the main, recording cracks, offset joints and root entry in PVC, clay and cast iron, so you see the exact distance and depth of the break.
