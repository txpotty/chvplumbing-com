    Relief Valve Testing in Sterling Heights

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. We lift the test lever and watch it reseat. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.

    Expansion Tank Service in Sterling Heights, MI

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Sterling Heights

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. Figure on half an hour or a bit more for the drain. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Small leaks at the dielectric nipples get noted on your report.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Sterling Heights

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. We match the new valve to the heater's input rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Sterling Heights

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. Those chunks cause the popping. When a plain flush will not move them, we use a descaling solution rated for potable systems, let it sit and then flush it all out. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Sterling Heights, MI

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. A pan under an attic tank is not optional. We confirm the pan drain line is open and runs to a safe spot. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Electric Element Check in Sterling Heights

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.
