    Why a Sewer Camera Inspection Belongs on Every Michigan Home Inspection

    By Andrew Blake · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Standard Home Inspection Misses

    A general home inspector runs the faucets and flushes the toilets, and that is about where the drain check ends. The sewer lateral, the pipe from the house to the public main or septic tank, never gets looked at. It is also the most expensive pipe on the property to replace. Sellers rarely know its condition.

    Our crews at CHV Plumbing inspect lines for buyers across Michigan, and the camera work rarely takes more than an hour. The fee is small next to a replacement. An empty house is the biggest risk, since a broken line can go unnoticed for months when nobody is using the drains.

    Basements are the norm, so sump pumps, drain tile and foundation drainage are a big part of residential plumbing.

    Older laterals were often clay tile or cast iron, and years of shifting soil and growing roots break both of them down. Roots get in where clay joints pull apart. Cast iron rusts along the bottom until the waste is running over dirt instead of pipe. Neither shows up from inside the house until there is a backup.

    What the Camera Shows

    The camera goes in through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and travels the full length of the line out to the main. A locator on the camera head lets us mark the exact spot and depth of any problem on the ground above. You get a recording. Here is what we look for.

    • Root intrusion at joints
    • Bellies, or sags, that hold standing water
    • Cracks, breaks and offset joints
    • Corroded or collapsed cast iron
    • Orangeburg pipe that has started to flatten
    • Grease buildup or debris from a past backup

    Septic rules are set mostly by local health departments rather than one statewide code, so requirements vary from one county to the next.

    On a septic property, the camera also shows the line from the house to the tank. That run gets missed often. A sag or root mass near the inlet baffle is common, and a pump-out alone will not do anything to fix it. Ask the seller for the last pump-out date and any drain field records. The drain field needs its own septic inspection, since a camera cannot judge it.

    Inspection Cost Versus Repair Cost

    Sewer line repairs range from a single spot repair to a full lateral replacement under the driveway. One spot repair can be a day's work, but replacing a full line under concrete may mean a week plus restoration. Once you own the house, that bill is yours. Before closing, it is a negotiating point.

    Buyers use the video in a few ways. Some ask for a price reduction or a repair credit at closing. Others ask the seller to have the repair done by a licensed plumber with a permit before closing, then rerun the camera to confirm it. The video is evidence.

    Scheduling an Inspection

    Book the camera inspection during your inspection period, not after. Most purchase contracts only give you a short window, often somewhere around 7 to 10 days, to raise any repair issues. You can reach CHV Plumbing at (810) 380-5918. It helps to have the address and the build year when you call. We can usually get a camera out within a couple of days, and we send the video to you and your agent the same day.
