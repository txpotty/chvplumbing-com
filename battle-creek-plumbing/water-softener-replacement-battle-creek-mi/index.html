    Scale in the water heater is the expensive part of hard water. Scale settles on the bottom of a tank heater and insulates the water from the burner, and tankless heaters clog even faster. A working softener stops that.

    Salt-Free Alternatives in Battle Creek, MI

    Some homeowners do not want salt or have no drain near the main line. A TAC conditioner is the honest alternative there, and we tell people plainly that it prevents scale but will not give them the slick feel of soft water. Sodium concerns are another reason.

    Replacing Pelican Water Systems in Battle Creek

    Pelican systems usually combine a carbon filter with either a salt-based softener or a salt-free conditioner in one setup on the main line. Depending on how old it is, we swap media or the full unit. Following the install specs keeps the warranty intact.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Replacement in Battle Creek

    Salt-based softeners pull calcium and magnesium out of the water by trading them for sodium on a bed of resin beads, which is the only method that actually makes water soft. Old clock-timer units come out and metered valves go in. Salt use usually drops. The brine setting gets matched to your hardness so the unit is not wasting salt with every cycle.

    Fleck Valve Programming in Battle Creek, MI

    Programming is where most Fleck replacements go wrong. The valve needs your hardness in grains, the number of people and the regeneration time set correctly, or it will regenerate too often or not enough. We set it before we leave.

    Undersized softeners are common in homes that added a bathroom or a couple of teenagers since the original system went in. A 32,000-grain unit that regenerated weekly for a family of three may be running dry every three days now. Sizing is based on current usage.
