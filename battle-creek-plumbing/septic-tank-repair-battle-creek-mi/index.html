    Soil decides how fast the field can take water, which decides how long it lasts. Freeze and thaw cycles shift the soil around buried pipes, which can crack older clay and cast iron sewer laterals. Heavy clay drains slowly and floods trenches after big rains, while very sandy or gravelly ground lets effluent through before it is cleaned. We dig a test hole before quoting field work.

    Some things, yes: you can clean an effluent filter with a garden hose if the riser comes up to grade. Work inside the tank is another matter, because hydrogen sulfide can drop a person in seconds. Leave pump wiring to a pro.

    Tee and pump repairs take two to four hours. A line repair with digging usually takes most of a day, and a field job can stretch over several days if the inspector must see it open. We give you the timeline at the diagnosis.

    We repair Zoeller and Liberty pumps nearly every week, along with SJE Rhombus alarm panels and the float switches that run them. Concrete and plastic tanks are familiar ground. Aerobic units from the common manufacturers are on the list too, along with their aerators and timers.

    Get the tank pumped every three to five years and have the effluent filter cleaned at the very same visit. Fix running toilets and dripping faucets right away. One bad flapper can send hundreds of gallons a day out to the trenches. Keep wipes out.

    The gas in a septic tank is mostly methane and hydrogen sulfide, and leaning over an open lid can knock you unconscious. Every year people die in tanks, usually reaching for a dropped tool. Concrete lids are also heavy enough to crush a hand. Leave the lid to us.
