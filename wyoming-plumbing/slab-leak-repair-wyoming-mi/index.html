    Slab Leak Reroute in Wyoming

    Older copper under a slab tends to leak again. A reroute stops the cycle. New PEX runs through the attic and down the walls while the old pipe stays buried, with openings placed behind vanities or in closets.

    Cold Water Slab Leak Repair in Wyoming

    Cold leaks don't make warm spots. The bill gives them away. Look for a rising bill, a meter that spins with nothing on or damp carpet, since a cold leak wets the soil as much as a hot one. Your Wyoming home is no exception. We isolate the cold line and repair or reroute it.

    Commercial Slab Leak Repair in Wyoming, MI

    Commercial slabs in Wyoming carry longer pipe runs and more branches. Locating takes longer. Work meets Michigan code and stays safe and compliant.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Repair in Wyoming, MI

    Hot lines leak under slabs more than cold lines. Expansion wears them out. The pipe swells and shrinks against rock every time hot water flows, and a recirculation pump keeps that going constantly, thinning the wall. We repair or reroute the line and can set the recirculation pump on a timer.

    Pipe Rerouting and Repiping in Wyoming

    Old galvanized or thin copper is hard to patch reliably. Rerouting skips it. We abandon the leaking run in place and install new pipe through walls or the attic, so the problem section is gone from service and the water travels through fresh line that you can reach later. Wyoming homes with original plumbing see this often.

    After-Hours Slab Leak Calls in Wyoming

    A main line splitting under the slab can lift tile and soak drywall fast. Don't wait until morning.     Ground Radar and Acoustic Locating in Wyoming

    Ground-penetrating radar shows what is inside the slab before we cut. Radar maps steel in the concrete. Combined with a listening device, radar finds leaks in any pipe type, even with traffic noise, since radar maps the route and the mic confirms the spot. We use both in Wyoming.

    Slab Leak Repair for PEX and Copper Lines in Wyoming, MI

    Copper and PEX fail in different ways under a slab. Copper tends to pinhole. Old copper wears through where it touches gravel or reacts with the water, while PEX usually leaks at a fitting that was crimped short or chewed by rodents near an access point. Trucks in Wyoming, MI carry parts for both.
