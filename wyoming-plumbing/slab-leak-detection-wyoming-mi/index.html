    Acoustic Slab Leak Detection in Wyoming

    Our technicians rely on highly sensitive acoustic listening devices to pinpoint the exact location of a leak under your slab. This method involves listening for the faint sound of escaping water, even through concrete. It's particularly effective for pressurized water lines, allowing us to find leaks without unnecessary excavation and minimizing disruption to your Wyoming home.

    Non-Invasive Leak Detection in Wyoming, MI

    Our primary goal is to locate your slab leak with the least amount of disruption possible. This approach saves your home in Wyoming, MI from unnecessary damage, preserves your landscaping and lowers the overall cost and time of the repair process.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Wyoming

    Rerouting abandons the leaking line under the slab and runs new pipe through the walls or attic. It avoids breaking concrete and often costs less than direct access under finished floors. We usually use PEX for reroutes because it handles heat and resists hard-water corrosion. This option is popular when the slab is covered in tile or hardwood.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Detection in Wyoming

    Hot water lines leak under slabs more often than cold lines because heat speeds up corrosion and causes expansion and contraction. Signs include warm spots on the floor, a water heater that runs constantly and higher gas or electric bills. Thermal imaging shows the heat pattern spreading through tile or concrete. We confirm with a pressure test and acoustic listening before flagging the spot.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes vulnerable to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures.     Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water with fixtures off, a moving water meter and an unexplained jump in your water bill are common signs. Cracked tile, damp carpet and mildew odors also suggest a slab leak. Call us if you notice any of these.

    Slab leaks can cause significant damage including foundation cracks, soil erosion beneath the slab, mold and mildew growth in wall cavities or under flooring, damage to flooring materials and increased humidity levels. Over time, it can lead to structural instability and major, costly repairs.
