    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Wyoming, MI

    Copper leaks are loud on a ground microphone. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. Both show up on our schedule every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX are the two leaks we find most.

    Leak Testing for Multifamily and Offices in Wyoming

    Multifamily buildings and office parks have long supply systems that let leaks hide for months. We isolate each unit or floor, test it and trace leaks with the same acoustic and thermal tools we use in single-family houses. Every unit gets its own report. Catching leaks early cuts tenant complaints and insurance claims across the building.

    Slab Leaks From Ground Movement in Wyoming

    Pipe set in concrete cannot flex when the ground moves, so it cracks at elbows and where it rises through the slab. Freeze and thaw cycles move the soil around buried pipes, which can crack older clay and cast iron sewer laterals. Isolating the line comes first. Then acoustic listening and thermal imaging find the leak without breaking up the floor to look, and you get a marked spot plus repair options before the saw comes out.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Wyoming, MI

    A ceiling stain right below a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. Our job is figuring out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.

    Commercial Building Leaks in Wyoming, MI

    Restaurants, offices and apartment complexes in Wyoming lose money every day a leak runs. Sprinkler lines, cooling tower makeup water and long copper mains are common sources, and a leak in any of them can run under a floor or above a drop ceiling for weeks. Each zone gets isolated and tested, then mark leaks for repair. Tenants usually never notice.

    Pool and Spa Leak Detection in Wyoming

    Heat and wind can pull a lot of water out of a pool, so it is hard to tell evaporation from a leak. A bucket test settles it. We then pressure test the plumbing lines and dye test around lights, skimmers and return fittings, because a running leak can undermine the soil around the pool.

    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Wyoming, MI

    The service line from the meter to the house can leak for months without surfacing, especially in old galvanized pipe. Green grass in a dry spell is a clue. We trace the line with an electronic locator, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak, and on plastic pipe we inject tracer gas and sniff for it at the surface.

    Gas Line Leak Locating in Wyoming

    Gas leaks are found by test, not by nose. Our plumbers pressure test the system with a gauge, and check every joint with soap and a detector. We also walk the yard line, since a dead strip of grass over a buried gas pipe often marks a leak that has been there a while. Utility coordination is included.
