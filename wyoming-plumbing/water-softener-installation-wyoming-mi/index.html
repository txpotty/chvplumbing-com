    Whole-House Water Softener Systems in Wyoming

    On a whole-house system, the softener goes on the main line after the shutoff and before the water heater. We add a bypass valve so you can isolate the softener for service, and we install a pressure regulator if your street pressure runs above 80 psi. Soft water reaches every fixture.     Softener Loop Installation in Wyoming

    No loop? A loop is the plumbing that routes the main line through a softener, and many older homes were built without one. We cut it in for you. Outdoor taps can stay on hard water, which saves salt. Future service gets easier too.

    Water Softener Replacement Services in Wyoming, MI

    Worn-out softeners usually show it in one of two ways, hard water at the tap or a brine tank that never drops. Our crew pulls the old system, checks the loop and drain line and installs a model matched to your water today. We haul away the old one. The work is usually done in a few hours.

    Water Softener Replacement in Wyoming, MI

    Older softeners lose ground as resin wears out, valve seals leak or injectors clog. While the old system is out, we inspect the loop and the drain, then install a newer metered unit. Salt use drops. You will notice soft water the same day.

    Reverse Osmosis Drinking Water Systems in Wyoming

    Reverse osmosis pushes water through a fine membrane that removes most dissolved solids, including the sodium a softener adds. Our RO installs go under the sink with their own faucet, and we can tie in the fridge or ice maker too. Pairs well with a softener. Filters get changed on schedule.

    Water Softener Replacement & Upgrades in Wyoming, MI

    Upgrading an old timer-based softener is one of the easiest ways to cut salt use. Newer valves meter actual water use, some connect to Wi-Fi for salt alerts and higher-capacity tanks regenerate less often for larger households. The savings add up. We swap it out in a few hours.

    Salt-Based Water Softener Systems in Wyoming, MI

    Salt-based softeners use ion exchange, swapping calcium and magnesium for sodium on a bed of resin beads. CHV Plumbing installs these with a properly sized resin tank and brine tank. A metered control valve regenerates only when needed, which keeps salt and water use down. They work best on very hard water.

    Combination Softener and Carbon Filter Systems in Wyoming, MI

    City water often carries chlorine or chloramine that affects taste and smell. Putting a carbon tank in front of the softener takes out chlorine and keeps it from wearing down the resin. You will taste it. We install twin-tank setups or dual-stage units.

    Sized and installed correctly, a softener should not noticeably drop your pressure. A pressure drop after install usually means the valve is too small or a filter is clogged.

    Soap scum on shower doors, dry skin, dull laundry and white scale on faucets are the classic signs. A quick hardness test at the sink tells us for sure. Testing is free.

    Salt use depends on hardness, water use and how efficient the softener is. A family of four with fairly hard water might use 40 to 80 pounds of salt per month, and a metered high-efficiency model uses less. Metering cuts salt use.

    Softeners remove hardness minerals using salt. Conditioners change how minerals behave to reduce scale, but softeners work better on very hard water.
