    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Wyoming

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Wyoming, MI

    With PEX-A, the tech slips a ring over the pipe end and stretches both with a battery tool.     Copper to PEX Conversions in Wyoming

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Well water across much of the state tends to be harder and often carries iron, which stains sinks and clogs aerators. A switch to PEX ends it.     Uponor PEX System Installation in Wyoming

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Wyoming

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it is how leaks start.
