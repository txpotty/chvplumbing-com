    Repiping Work We Handle in Wyoming, MI

    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Wyoming

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. It holds up well, does not mind sunlight in an exposed run and has a long track record behind it. It costs more. We sweat joints with lead-free solder or press them, hang the pipe at proper support intervals and test the whole system before patching.

    Copper Repiping in Wyoming, MI

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Copper takes a bit longer. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Wyoming

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. We count fixtures, check attic and crawlspace access, look at where the main comes in and ask about plans for future remodels before we draw a route. Then we size the lines. Bigger homes may need a 1-inch trunk so the shower does not drop when the dishwasher fills.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Wyoming, MI

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Brown water after the house sits overnight, weak flow at upstairs showers and leaks at threaded joints are the usual signs that galvanized is near the end. We replace it with PEX or copper. Owners usually notice stronger flow the first day.

    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Wyoming, MI

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. One slab leak with access and repair often costs $1,000 to $4,000. The numbers add up quickly. After two or three leaks, a repipe often pays for itself, and we give you a side-by-side comparison so you can make that call with real numbers in front of you.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Wyoming

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Many homes built from the 1980s through the early 2000s have one or the other. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Wyoming, MI

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Weak flow and rusty water are common. We remove or abandon the galvanized lines and replace them with PEX or copper, and your water pressure and water quality usually improve the same day the new lines go into service.
