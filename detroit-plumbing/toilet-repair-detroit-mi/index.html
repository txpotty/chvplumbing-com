    Emergency Toilet Repair in Detroit, MI

    An overflowing bowl in a one-bathroom house is an emergency, and so is a burst supply line spraying water across the floor at full house pressure. Shut the angle stop first. Turn the valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it is tight, then call us so we can walk you through the rest. The first goal on site is stopping the water, then a permanent repair if the part is on the truck.

    Toilet Flush Repair in Detroit, MI

    Two or three tries to get a full flush points to a flapper closing too early, a chain hooked in the wrong hole, scale in the rim jets, or a cracked overflow tube letting water bypass the bowl. Each one has a tell. We watch a full cycle with the lid off and replace the flush valve if the overflow tube is cracked, since it cannot be patched.

    Running Toilet Repair in Detroit

    A tank that keeps hissing and refilling every few minutes is usually leaking past the flapper or overfilling into the overflow tube. It adds up fast. We look at the chain, float, flapper and seat before touching a part, then replace only what failed. Figure on thirty minutes for a typical running toilet, longer if the flush valve seat is pitted.

    Commercial Toilet Repair in Detroit

    Office and retail restrooms take more flushes in a day than a house sees in a month, so the parts wear faster and fail harder. Commercial bowls usually run on flushometer valves rather than tanks, and those need diaphragm kits and vacuum breakers from time to time. We carry Sloan and Zurn kits. If downtime is a problem, we can come in before you open or after you lock up.

    Flush Mechanism Repair in Detroit, MI

    A weak flush usually means the tank is not filling to the fill line, the chain has too much slack, or the flapper is closing before the tank empties. We set the water level to the mark on the overflow tube and adjust the chain to about half an inch of slack. Handles break too. A loose or cracked handle is a five-minute swap.

    Fill Valve Replacement in Detroit

    Old ballcock valves with a brass arm and a big plastic float are loud and slow to refill, and they tend to stick open when scale builds up in the ports. We replace them with an adjustable modern fill valve from Fluidmaster or Korky and set the height so the critical level mark sits at least an inch above the overflow. It is a twenty-minute job. You will notice the difference the first night, because the tank stops hissing.

    Water coming through the ceiling under an upstairs bathroom usually traces back to a failed wax ring or a cracked supply nut, and the drywall below holds the evidence. Stop using that toilet. The toilet comes up, the flange gets checked, and the bowl goes back on a new seal, then we show you how far the water traveled so you can decide about the ceiling.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Repair in Detroit

    Most simple toilet repairs, a new flapper or fill valve, cost about $125 to $275 installed. Pulling and resetting a toilet on a fresh wax ring usually runs $175 to $350, and a flange repair can bring the total to $300 to $500. You approve the flat price before we pick up a tool.

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber, Mansfield and Delta cover most of what we see, and we stock their common flappers and fill valves. Older brands like Eljer and Briggs are fine too. A discontinued model may need a part ordered, which usually takes a day or two.

    Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down by hand, which stops more water from reaching the bowl right away. Then close the angle stop. It is the small valve on the wall behind the toilet, and it turns clockwise. With the water off, try a flange plunger on the clog, and call us if it does not clear.

    Watch for phantom refills at night, a hiss from the tank, a toilet that shifts when you sit, and stains or soft flooring around the base. Repeated clogs belong on that list too. Catching these early keeps a flapper job from turning into a subfloor repair.

    We do. Eljer, Briggs, Crane and Universal Rundle toilets still show up in older homes, and while some of their flush valves take odd parts, adapters and universal kits cover a good share of them. When a part is no longer made, we say so and price the alternatives.

    A loose chain or a float set too high is a fair DIY fix, and so is swapping a flapper if you buy the right size for your flush valve. Take the old one to the store. If the toilet still runs after that, the seat or the fill valve is usually the problem, and that is a good time to call CHV Plumbing.

    Repeat clogs usually come from wipes, toys, too much paper per flush, or an early low-flow toilet that never moved waste well. Sometimes the cause is deeper, like a sagging drain, a crushed pipe, roots in the sewer or a blocked vent that keeps the bowl from draining with full force. Cameras settle the question.

    Swapping tank parts or resetting a toilet on a new wax seal generally does not need a permit. Relocating a drain or adding a fixture typically does, and the rules come from local code, so they vary from one town to the next even within Michigan. We look it up before work begins.
