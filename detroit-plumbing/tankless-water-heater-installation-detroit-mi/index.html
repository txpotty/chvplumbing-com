    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Detroit, MI

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. The vent changes as well. We plan both on the quote.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Detroit

    Upgrading from a tank in Detroit begins with your current setup: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits.     Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Detroit, MI

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Detroit, MI homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Detroit

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job since the tank's location may not suit the new unit. A garage or outside wall near the gas meter can shorten the gas run and simplify venting, but it may lengthen the hot water run to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Detroit

    Propane homes in Detroit are good candidates for tankless, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. A small tank can run short.
