    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Replacement in Detroit

    Costs vary by job. A tank water heater replacement usually runs $1,400 to $3,000, a water softener $1,500 to $3,500, a water line $1,800 to $5,500 and a sewer line $4,000 to $15,000 or more.

    Hard water causes scale and sediment, while heat and shifting soil stress pipes and equipment. Homes around Detroit on private wells usually benefit from a water test before picking a softener, since mineral levels vary widely. Regular maintenance helps, but replacement sooner or later becomes the smarter choice.

    The timeline depends on the complexity of the job. A water heater replacement usually takes 2 to 4 hours. A major project like a full house repipe or sewer line replacement can take 1 to 5 days. We will provide an estimated timeframe during your consultation.

    We primarily use PEX (cross-linked polyethylene) and copper piping for water supply lines. For sewer and drain lines, we use durable PVC (polyvinyl chloride) or ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) pipes, based on the application and local codes.

    Consider the age of the equipment, the cost of repair compared with replacement and how often it has needed service. We give you both options with pricing so you can decide.

    Replacing old pipes with PEX or copper offers many benefits. PEX is flexible, corrosion-resistant and less prone to bursting in freezes, while copper is durable and long-lasting. Both improve water quality, eliminate leaks from old pipes and can noticeably boost water pressure and flow throughout your Detroit home.

    CHV Plumbing stands behind our plumbing replacement services with comprehensive warranties covering both parts and labor. The warranty period varies by the type of replacement and the manufacturer's warranty on components, but we always give you clear information on your coverage for peace of mind.

    While some minor plumbing repairs can be DIY, major replacements like water heaters, sewer lines or repiping should always be handled by licensed professionals. These jobs involve complex systems, potential safety hazards (gas, high water pressure) and strict code compliance. Improper installation can lead to leaks, damage and even severe safety risks, costing more in the long run.
