    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Maintenance in Detroit

    The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that sacrifices itself to keep the steel tank from corroding. It attracts corrosive elements in the water. Regular inspection and replacement of a depleted anode rod are crucial for extending the lifespan of your water heater, particularly where water quality varies.

    Popping or rumbling noises, lukewarm water, rusty or smelly hot water, and slow recovery times are common signs.

    While some basic tasks like flushing a tank might seem DIY-friendly, professional maintenance is strongly recommended. Our technicians have the right tools, knowledge, and experience to perform thorough safety checks, identify potential hazards, and meet local codes, which a DIY approach might miss.

    During a water heater flush, we drain the tank completely and then refill it, letting water flow out through the drain valve until it runs clear. This process removes mineral deposits and sediment that collect at the bottom of the tank, which can hurt efficiency and cause damage.

    Sediment buildup consists of mineral deposits, mainly calcium and magnesium, that settle at the bottom of your water heater tank. Homes around Detroit on private wells usually benefit from a water test before picking a softener, since mineral levels vary widely.
