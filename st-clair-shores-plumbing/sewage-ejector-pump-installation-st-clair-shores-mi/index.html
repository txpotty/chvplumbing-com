    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause premature failure.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional inspect your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can catch potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is particularly important for older units.

    Pump replacements typically take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is built specifically to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve different purposes.

    Most ejector pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. Flushing wipes or other non-flushable items can shorten that life considerably.
