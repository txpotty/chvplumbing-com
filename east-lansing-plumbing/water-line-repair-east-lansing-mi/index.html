    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in East Lansing, MI

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. Once one spot leaks, the pipe on either side is usually just as thin. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Emergency Water Line Repair in East Lansing, MI

    First thing on a burst line is shutting the water off, at the house valve if it works and at the curb stop if it does not. Water pouring into a yard undermines everything around it. CHV Plumbing sends a plumber with a curb key, a pump and repair couplings. Most breaks are shut down quickly. If the repair will run long, a temporary bypass keeps water flowing to the house during the repair.

    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in East Lansing

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Many homes in Michigan were built before the 1970s and still have galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic gear and thermal imaging. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in East Lansing

    A trench through a paver driveway or a mature garden can cost more to restore than the pipe itself. That is where trenchless pays off. We dig a pit at each end and pull HDPE or PEX through with a boring tool or pipe puller, either along the old route or through fresh soil next to it. Most replacements finish in a day. You also get the manufacturer warranty on the new pipe.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in East Lansing, MI

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Homes around East Lansing on private wells usually benefit from a water test before picking a filtration system, since mineral levels vary widely. Scale builds up inside and the opening shrinks. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Usually a one-day job.

    Main Water Line Repair in East Lansing

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Commercial Water Line Repair in East Lansing, MI

    Commercial service lines are often 1 1/2 to 4 inch pipe feeding a meter bank, a backflow assembly and sometimes a fire line. No water means no open sign. CHV Plumbing can schedule repairs early in the morning or after closing, and we bring the larger fittings and couplings on the truck. We also arrange the backflow test and inspection that most water providers require after work on the commercial side of the meter.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in East Lansing

    Copper and PEX fail in different places. Copper tends to pinhole where a rock rubs it or where the water chemistry eats the wall from inside. PEX usually fails at a fitting. We cut out the bad section of copper and sweat or press in a new length, and for PEX we use the matching crimp or expansion tool so the joint is made the way the manufacturer intends.
