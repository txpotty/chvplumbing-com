    Pipe Bursting in East Lansing, MI

    Pipe bursting is used when the old pipe is too damaged to line or needs a larger diameter. A bursting head is pulled through the old pipe, fracturing it outward while a new HDPE pipe follows behind. The new pipe is fused into one continuous length with no joints. Bursting requires an entry and exit pit but avoids digging the full length.

    Cured-in-Place Pipe Lining in East Lansing

    CIPP lining involves cleaning the old pipe, then inserting a flexible liner saturated with epoxy resin. The liner is inflated against the pipe wall and cured with hot water, steam or UV light. Once hardened, it forms a smooth, jointless pipe inside the old one. Lining works best for cracks, root intrusion and separated joints.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in East Lansing

    The cost of trenchless pipe repair in East Lansing, MI can vary widely, usually ranging from $80 to $200 per linear foot, depending on the pipe's diameter, length of the repair, and the specific trenchless method used. While often higher initially than traditional digging, it usually saves money in the long run by avoiding extensive landscaping and property restoration costs. A full estimate will be provided after an inspection.

    While trenchless methods are highly versatile, they are not suitable for all types of pipe damage. Severely collapsed pipes, pipes with large offsets, or pipes that have suffered extreme structural failure may still require traditional excavation. A professional camera inspection from CHV Plumbing will determine what is possible.

    Most trenchless pipe repair projects, especially CIPP lining, can be completed in one to three days. Pipe bursting might take slightly longer, depending on the pipe length and complexity. This is much faster than traditional methods, which can take weeks due to excavation and restoration.

    Most common pipe materials, including cast iron, clay, PVC, and concrete, can be repaired using trenchless methods. However, the condition of the existing pipe is a factor. If a pipe is completely collapsed or badly misaligned, pipe bursting might be a more suitable trenchless option than lining. Our inspection will identify the best approach.

    Signs you might need trenchless pipe repair include recurring drain clogs, sewage backups, foul odors from drains, wet spots in your yard or foundation without visible leaks, and oddly lush patches of grass. These often point to a damaged or leaking underground pipe.

    Trenchless pipe repair is a complex process that takes specialized equipment, training, and expertise. It is not a DIY project. Attempting to repair underground pipes yourself can lead to further damage, costly mistakes, and potential safety hazards. Always trust a licensed plumber like CHV Plumbing.
