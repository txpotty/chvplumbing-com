Proper testing avoids replacing parts that are still good.

    Tankless Water Heater Descaling in East Lansing

Most manufacturers recommend descaling at least once a year in hard water areas. Regular service keeps flow rates and temperatures consistent.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Maintenance in East Lansing

    The cost of water heater maintenance in East Lansing, MI can range from $120 to $250, based on the type of water heater (tank or tankless) and the services required. This typically includes a full flush, anode rod inspection, and safety checks. More complex issues or parts replacement would be an additional cost.

    Yes, our workmanship and any parts we install are covered by a written warranty.

    The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that sacrifices itself to keep the steel tank from corroding. It attracts corrosive elements in the water. Regular inspection and replacement of a depleted anode rod are crucial for extending the lifespan of your water heater, particularly where water quality varies.

    Most tank flushes and inspections take about 45 to 90 minutes. Tankless descaling usually takes 1 to 2 hours.

    Absolutely. Regular maintenance greatly extends the lifespan of your water heater by preventing corrosion, sediment buildup, and other issues that lead to premature failure. It can add several years to your unit's working life.

    Sediment buildup consists of mineral deposits, mainly calcium and magnesium, that settle at the bottom of your water heater tank. Lake-sourced municipal water in Michigan is moderately hard, so scale gradually builds up in water heaters and fixtures. Sediment cuts efficiency, causes strange noises, and can lead to premature tank failure if not flushed regularly.

    Popping or rumbling noises, lukewarm water, rusty or smelly hot water, and slow recovery times are common signs.

    During a water heater flush, we drain the tank completely and then refill it, letting water flow out through the drain valve until it runs clear. This process removes mineral deposits and sediment that collect at the bottom of the tank, which can hurt efficiency and cause damage.
