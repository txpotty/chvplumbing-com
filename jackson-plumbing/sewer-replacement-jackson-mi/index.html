    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Jackson

    Pipe bursting pulls a new HDPE pipe through the old line while breaking the old pipe apart. It needs only small access pits at each end. This protects landscaping, driveways and patios. It works well for clay, cast iron and Orangeburg pipe. Most jobs are completed in one day.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Jackson

    Cast iron sewer pipe can corrode from the inside, creating rough surfaces and holes. Scale buildup catches debris and causes clogs. We replace cast iron with PVC or HDPE. This restores flow and eliminates corrosion. Many homes built before 1975 have cast iron lines.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Jackson, MI

    Clay pipe joints let roots in, while Orangeburg pipe made from tar paper deforms over time. Both are prone to failure. We replace these lines with modern materials. Camera inspection shows the extent of damage. Replacement ends recurring root problems.

    Commercial Sewer Replacement in Jackson

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Replacement in Jackson

    The cost of sewer replacement in Jackson varies widely, generally ranging from $3,000 to $25,000 or more, depending on factors like the length of the line, depth, soil conditions, method (trenchless vs. traditional), and material used.     Frequent backups, slow drains throughout the house, sewage odors and soggy yard areas are typical signs. Camera inspection verifies the problem.

    After replacement, avoid planting large trees or shrubs near the sewer line's path. Be mindful of what goes down your drains, and avoid flushing grease, wipes, and harsh chemicals. Regular professional inspections, especially if you have older pipes, can also help catch potential issues early.

    Sewer line replacement is a complex and dangerous job that should only be performed by licensed and experienced professionals. It is strongly recommended to hire a professional plumbing company like CHV Plumbing.

    For sewer replacement, we mainly use durable and long-lasting materials like PVC (polyvinyl chloride) and ABS (acrylonitrile butadiene styrene) pipes.
