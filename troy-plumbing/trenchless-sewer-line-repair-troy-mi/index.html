    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Troy, MI

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, lining beats breaking up the floor in two rooms.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Replacement in Troy

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. It goes oval and blisters, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Vitrified clay that still holds shape lines well.

    Sectional Point Repair in Troy

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. We push it in on a packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. One crack, one patch. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Pipe Bursting in Troy, MI

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. A 60-foot pull often takes under an hour.

    Cost Comparison of Trenchless vs Traditional in Troy, MI

    Depth changes the math. A line two feet down in open lawn is often cheaper to dig than to line, but a lateral eight feet deep that runs under the driveway and a porch costs far more to excavate than to burst or line from two pits. We quote both ways. Then you pick.

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow Trenchless Systems in Troy

    Perma-Liner and NuFlow both make lining systems we use, each with its own resin chemistry and installation equipment.     Causes of Sewer Line Failure in Troy, MI

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Freeze and thaw cycles shift the soil around buried pipes, which can crack older clay and cast iron sewer laterals. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Age does the rest. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under the weight of soil and traffic.
