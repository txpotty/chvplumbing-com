    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Troy

    Standard drain snaking in Troy, MI usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Mains with heavy roots, or jobs that need jetting, run $400 to $1,000 and sometimes more. A camera inspection adds to the bill. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the typical signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. Take that one seriously. Repeat clogs in one line usually mean a deeper problem, often roots at a joint well out in the yard.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Hair strainers in tubs and showers help. Run hot water after using the disposal, and scrape plates into the trash before rinsing so less food goes down the kitchen line. Root-prone lines need cutting every year or two.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Jetting scours the whole pipe with high-pressure water and strips the grease, scale and sludge a cable slides past. For recurring clogs, jetting is the better bet.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. We tell you up front. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Every rooter job from CHV Plumbing carries a guarantee for a set period on the line we cleared. We go over the terms before we start. Coverage is specific. If that same line stops up again within the guarantee window, we come back and clear it at no extra charge.

    Clay or older cast iron lines with trees nearby usually need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera look can set the timing. Seeing how fast roots came back since the last cutting tells us whether to plan a yearly visit or stretch it to two years.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.
