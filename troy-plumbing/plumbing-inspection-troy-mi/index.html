    What a Plumbing Inspection Covers in Troy, MI

    Fixture, Drain and Shutoff Valve Check in Troy, MI

    We run every faucet, shower and toilet and look for slow drains, leaks and weak flow. We test each valve. Angle stops and the main shutoff valve often seize over the years, and we also listen for gurgling drains that point to venting problems, since small fixes like these are easy to plan once you know about them.

    Pre-Purchase Inspection With Sewer Video in Troy

    Plumbing problems are some of the most expensive surprises after closing. Our pre-purchase inspection covers the supply pipe type, the sewer condition on video, the water heater's age and every fixture. We send the report to your agent too. Buyers often use the findings to request repairs or credits, and the sewer video by itself can head off a very costly surprise.

    Seasonal Plumbing Checks in Troy, MI

    Long, cold winters with heavy snow are the defining stress on plumbing in Michigan, and the heating season can stretch for many months. A seasonal check looks at the parts of the system that weather affects most. Outdoor faucets, exposed pipe, irrigation backflow devices and the heater drain and relief valve all get checked. Insulation gets checked too. Swapping a cracked outdoor faucet or insulating a pipe costs little next to a burst line in the wall.

    Water Heater Inspections in Troy

    Water heaters give clues long before they fail. We read the serial number for age, look for rust at the fittings and the base, check the venting and test the T and P relief valve. Tankless units get checked for scale. On gas units, we look at the burner and flame, check for a sediment trap and confirm the vent is drafting so combustion gases go outside.

    Commercial Plumbing Inspections in Troy, MI

    Commercial buildings have equipment homes do not, so our commercial inspections cover more ground. Backflow devices, grease traps, large heaters, flushometers and the main sewer are all part of it. Records matter for inspectors. Managers use the report to fix problems before an inspector or water provider flags them.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Troy

    Most home plumbing inspections run $150 to $350, depending on the size and age of the house. Camera work costs extra. Adding a sewer camera inspection raises the total, and we tell you the full price before the visit so there is nothing new on the invoice when we finish.

    We check water supply lines, drain lines, fixtures, the water heater, sump pump, garbage disposal and exposed gas lines. We test how drains flow. Our plumber also checks for leaks, corrosion and vent issues, measures pressure and writes up the condition of every component.

    Our technicians inspect all major water heater brands, including Rheem, AO Smith and Bradford White. Tankless is included. We also check Rinnai and Noritz tankless heaters and the common fixture brands found in most homes, so the brand on your equipment will not limit what we can inspect.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Camera work takes longer. Larger homes, long sewer lines or a hidden leak that needs tracing can push it longer, and we will tell you the expected time when you book so you can plan around it.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Noisy drains are a clue. A damp wall, a musty cabinet or a warm spot on the floor can mean a hidden leak, and an inspection finds it before it spreads.

    You can do a basic check by looking under sinks and watching your water meter. A plumber goes further. Professionals bring sewer video, pressure readings, leak tracing and code knowledge, which find what a visual look misses.

    Our full inspection covers supply pipe, drains, fixtures, shutoff valves, the water heater, gas connectors and the sewer line. Pressure gets measured. A meter leak test, a check for slab leak signs and hard water damage and a look at any softener or filter are also included.

    A one to two year routine works for most homes. Older homes and properties with past leaks may benefit from yearly inspections.
