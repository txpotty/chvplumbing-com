    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Bay City, MI

    Cast iron drains and sewer lines corrode from the inside, leaving rough scale, cracks and channel rot along the bottom of the pipe. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or cutters, then line it to restore a smooth, sealed interior. This is especially useful for cast iron running under slabs where excavation is costly. Lining can add decades of service.

    Pipe Bursting Sewer Line Replacement in Bay City

    Pipe bursting is a trenchless method used for full sewer line replacement. It involves pulling a new pipe through the old one while breaking apart the existing pipe. This creates a seamless, durable replacement, often using high-density polyethylene pipe, which is highly resistant to corrosion and root intrusion, common problems in older Bay City homes. This method is ideal when the existing pipe is severely damaged or collapsed.

    Trenchless vs Traditional Sewer Repair Costs in Bay City, MI

    While the upfront cost of trenchless sewer repair in Bay City, MI can sometimes be comparable to or slightly higher than traditional methods, the overall savings are often substantial. This is because trenchless repairs eliminate the need for extensive excavation, backfilling and landscape restoration. Homeowners save money on repairing driveways, patios and gardens, making it a more sensible choice in the long run for many.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Relining in Bay City, MI

    CIPP relining creates a new pipe within an existing damaged sewer line. A resin-saturated felt tube is inserted into the old pipe and then inflated, curing in place to form a smooth, durable and watertight liner. This technique is excellent for repairing cracks, leaks and minor offsets without needing to dig up the entire line. It is a fast and effective solution for many common sewer problems faced by Michigan homeowners.

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Bay City

    Cured-in-place lining creates a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line, then pull or invert a resin-saturated liner into place and cure it with hot water, steam or UV light. The result is a seamless, jointless pipe that roots cannot get into. Lining works well for cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their shape.
