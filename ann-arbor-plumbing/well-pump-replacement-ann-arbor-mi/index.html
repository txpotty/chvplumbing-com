    What We Fix on Well Systems in Ann Arbor, MI

    Well Pump Brands We Install in Ann Arbor, MI

    Goulds, Grundfos, Franklin Electric and Sta-Rite cover nearly every well pump we install. We pick by the well, not the logo. Parts for Goulds and Franklin are easy to get, which matters when a control box dies on a weekend.

    Agricultural and Ranch Well Pumps in Ann Arbor

    Irrigation and stock wells often run 3 to 10 HP pumps, sometimes on three-phase power with a motor starter and overload protection at the panel. We replace those pumps and the starters that protect them. We schedule around watering times.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Ann Arbor

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Most fail from sand chewing up the impellers or a lightning surge that cooks the windings. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. The reading shows whether the fault is the motor or the wire.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Ann Arbor, MI

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. A pinhole in the suction pipe lets air in, the pump loses prime, and it runs dry until the seal burns up. The suction line gets a pressure test. If the well is too deep for a jet pump, we will say so and quote a submersible.

    Emergency Well Pump Replacement in Ann Arbor

    Dry faucets on a well-only property mean no flushing or cooking, so we treat those as same-day calls whenever the crew and the weather allow. We keep 1/2 and 3/4 HP submersibles on the truck. We also bring splice kits and switches.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Ann Arbor, MI

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. The rest is labor, wire, pipe and fittings, plus the hoist time to get it all down the hole. Every line is on the quote.

    Well Pump Efficiency Upgrades in Ann Arbor, MI

    Older 1 HP pumps running against a 60 psi switch use more power than most people expect, especially on a well with a long horizontal run to the house. A correctly sized pump, or a variable-speed drive, can cut the run time. Not always worth it. We will show you the payback.

    Well Pump Sizing in Ann Arbor

    Sizing starts with the well log. We get depth and static level from the well log or a sounder, then a draw-down test shows what the well can really produce in gallons per minute. Pumps bigger than the well can supply run dry.

    Wells around Ann Arbor vary a lot in depth, even on the same road, so the last pump we pulled down the street tells us only so much about yours. Your well log is the starting point. A sounder fills in when there is no log.
