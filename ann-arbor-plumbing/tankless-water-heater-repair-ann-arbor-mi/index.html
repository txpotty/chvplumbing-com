    Gas Tankless Water Heater Repair in Ann Arbor

    Pressure at full fire matters on a gas tankless burner, not just at the moment it lights. We put a manometer on the inlet test port and run every hot tap in the house, since pressure that sags under load leads to flame loss and slugs of cold water. Then we check the exhaust. Codes come off when the numbers are right.

    Rinnai Error Code Repair in Ann Arbor

    Rinnai owners usually call about a code 10, which points to an air supply or exhaust blockage. We check the vent terminal for bird nests and dryer lint, then pull the front cover to inspect the intake screen and the combustion fan. A code 10 can also come from a vent run that is too long for the model. We measure.

    Emergency Tankless Water Heater Repair in Ann Arbor, MI

    A tankless heater that dies on a cold morning in Ann Arbor is an emergency for a family of five. We take no-hot-water calls first, and the truck carries the parts that fail most: flame rods, igniters, flow sensors, thermistors and inlet filters. A leaking heat exchanger gets isolated at the service valves and the part goes on order that day. Control boards take longer.

    Igniter and Flame Rod Repair in Ann Arbor, MI

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. An oxidized flame rod gives a weak microamp reading and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, look for a cracked igniter or a lazy spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Repair in Ann Arbor, MI

    Whole-house electric tankless units pull a lot of current, often 100 to 150 amps on two or three dedicated breakers. When a breaker trips under load, the cause can be a shorted element or a loose, scorched lug, and both get tested with a meter before anything is replaced. Element resistance and supply voltage get measured. Scorched wire gets cut back.

    Tankless Repair vs Replacement Cost in Ann Arbor, MI

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350, and sensor or igniter repairs land between $250 and $600. A heat exchanger out of warranty can cost $800 to $1,500 in parts and labor. A new unit costs a lot more. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.

    Commercial Tankless Repair in Ann Arbor, MI

    Hotels and gyms draw hot water from open to close, so their tankless racks scale up two or three times faster than a home unit. We service rack systems with manifold kits, pulling the fault log and testing the flow sensor on every heater in the rack, and set a descaling schedule by run hours. Quarterly is common.

    Navien Condensate and Fan Repair in Ann Arbor

    Navien's E016 is a heat exchanger overheat, and scale or a backed-up condensate line is usually behind it. We also read fan speed from the service menu and compare it to spec, since a fan that cannot reach target RPM will cause flame and exhaust faults on the same unit. We clean the trap and screen, then retest.
