    Expansion Tank Installation in Ann Arbor, MI

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Heater Installation in Ann Arbor

    The cost of water heater installation in Ann Arbor, MI can range from $1,200 to $4,000 or more, depending on the type of unit (traditional tank vs. tankless), its capacity, fuel type (gas or electric) and any needed plumbing or electrical upgrades. Tankless units usually have higher installation costs due to more complex venting and utility requirements. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after assessing your specific needs.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by gallons per minute.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, water heater installation involves working with gas lines, high voltage electricity and water connections, all of which carry significant risks if not handled correctly. Improper installation can lead to leaks, gas explosions, electrical fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It can also void your warranty, and local codes often require a licensed plumber for this work.

    To improve efficiency, ensure your new water heater is properly sized for your home, consider a tankless model for on-demand heating, insulate hot water pipes and set the thermostat to 120°F (49°C) to prevent scalding and reduce energy consumption.

    The typical lifespan of a traditional tank water heater is about 10 to 15 years, while tankless water heaters can last 20 years or more with proper maintenance. Factors like local water quality, maintenance frequency and usage habits can all affect the actual lifespan of your unit. Regular flushing can help extend its life.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for water heater replacement. We pull permits when required and confirm the install meets code.

    A standard tank replacement typically takes two to four hours. Tankless installs or relocations can take most of a day.

    The decision to repair or replace often depends on the age of your unit, the cost of the repair and its energy efficiency. If your water heater is over 10 years old, often breaks down, or the repair cost is more than 50% of a new unit, replacement is often the more economical choice. Newer models also offer much better energy efficiency.
